# Three openings (about four minutes each)

Optional — the [format guide](format-guide.md#block-1--opening-010) works without them. They are three worked versions of the opening, minutes 3–10: they start **after** the wall. Always begin at the wall, not with yourself: point at the three questions and ask, *"These three questions come from you. Which one burns most today?"* Let two or three people answer. Then pick one variant and say it in your own cadence.

No show of hands in any variant — plan and self-test status came quietly with the pre-workshop form. The only question to the room comes in the Foundation block: *"Who would like a neighbour in the lab?"*

Replace each placeholder with one sentence that happened to you. Delete any placeholder you cannot fill honestly. Say the pattern line under it instead, and say that it is a pattern you have heard — not your story. Do not name a chapter, in the room or outside it.

The variants are written for a trainer who has been a chapter chair and holds a regional role — the profile the format was designed around. Coming from another role? Keep the lens you have, say the pattern line for the other, and see [format-guide.md § Your two roles](format-guide.md#your-two-roles--speaking-from-both-without-talking-down). A regional role reports on chapters, not on Chapter Managers. Every Chapter Manager in the room works for their chapter — self-employed or employed by it, never by YPO Global. They use their own WhatsApp number. You are a guest at their desk.

**In a guest room** (another region invited you): your regional role is only "a regional officer in another region", one clause. Replace the sentence about what goes back to the region with *"The anonymous sheet goes to {{HOST}}, not to me."* See [format-guide.md § Running it as a guest](format-guide.md#running-it-as-a-guest).

## A — Both seats, then the sentence

Thank you for those answers — we will come back to each of them. About me, one sentence: I have sat on the other side of your desk, [Trainer: your roles in one clause, e.g. as a past chapter chair and in a regional role], and I still do not know your job as well as you do.

From my year in the chair, one thing is still with me. [Trainer: own example as chair]

If that bracket is empty, say this and call it a pattern: *the handover was one conversation, and the dates slipped after the officers changed.*

From the regional seat, one sentence, with no chapter on it. [Trainer: own observation as regional officer]

If that bracket is empty, say this and call it a pattern: *the same question gets asked in many chapters each year, because the answer still lives in one person's head.*

Please hear this once, so you do not have to wonder later. Nothing I hear today goes back to any chapter with a name on it. What I take to the region is patterns, and an anonymous feedback sheet.

The afternoon is one sentence. Chairs and officers rotate every year. The Chapter Manager stays. AI helps you become — and keep — the memory of the chapter, without more hours. That last part is a bet, not a promise: the hours come from searching and answering twice. Test it with your own week — your log will tell you.

Nothing gets approved today. Every "that won't work for us" goes on the wall — that is how v0.2 gets written.

We build one assistant and use it three ways. What we do not finish goes home with the kit.

## B — A pattern I contributed to

Thank you — every one of those has a block this afternoon. About me, one sentence: [Trainer: your roles in one clause], and I have sat on the other side of your desk.

What repeats across chapters, as a pattern, is this shape: one Chapter Manager, and several officers, each with an urgent request on a different channel. [Trainer: own observation as regional officer]

If that bracket is empty, keep the shape and call it a pattern you have heard. The kit has its own answer to it, written as guardrails a chapter can adopt: one channel, event work budgeted apart from the base agreement, and a briefing before a new officer starts.

I know that shape from the chair's side, because I made someone's job harder. [Trainer: own example as chair]

If that bracket is empty, say this and call it a pattern, not a confession you are inventing: *requests left the board by several routes, and the Chapter Manager had to guess what mattered today.*

No chapter in this room will be my example. My regional role reports on chapters, not on you; nothing I hear today goes back with a name on it.

I am not here to tell you how to do the work. You already know it better than I do. You work for your chapter — self-employed or employed by it, never by YPO Global. Your WhatsApp number stays yours. What the chapter needs to keep has to land in a folder the chapter owns — a summary, a decision, a file — because the chats go with you.

The idea we will actually use is one sentence. Chairs and officers rotate every year. The Chapter Manager stays. AI, on a paid plan with training on your data switched off, helps you keep that memory without more hours — and that part is a bet you can test with your own week. The assistant drafts. You send.

Nothing gets approved today. Every "that won't work for us" goes on the wall — that is how v0.2 gets written.

## C — Your questions, my two seats

Those are your words on the wall, and the afternoon follows them. About me, one sentence: I have sat on the other side of your desk — [Trainer: your roles in one clause].

Here is the sentence I trust from both seats. Chairs and officers rotate every year. The Chapter Manager stays. AI helps you become — and keep — the memory of the chapter, without more hours.

I trust it from the chair's seat because of one year I actually had. [Trainer: own example as chair]

If that bracket is empty, use the pattern and label it: *what I needed from my Chapter Manager lived in their head, and the handover did not catch it.*

I trust it from the regional seat because the same gap shows up as a pattern, not as a ranking of chapters. [Trainer: own observation as regional officer]

If that bracket is empty, use this pattern: *three places to file things is a pain that comes up again and again; one chapter folder, with a fixed structure, is the fix this kit builds.*

"Without more hours" is a bet, not a promise. The hours come from searching and answering twice. Test it with your own week — your log will tell you.

You work for your chapter — self-employed or as its employee — on your own number. I will not tell you how you should work. There is a page in the kit you can take to your chair — one channel, a paid plan or a budget line, role accounts — if and when it fits. Your chair decides. I do not take a problem with a board back to anyone unless you ask me, afterwards, one to one. Nothing from today goes back to a chapter with a name.

Nothing gets approved today. Every "that won't work for us" goes on the wall — that is how v0.2 gets written. What is said about a chapter stays in this room; the screens use fictional material.

Then we build.
