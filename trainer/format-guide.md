# Format guide — Operational AI Excellence for Chapter Managers

Purpose: everything you need to run the 120-minute workshop for Chapter Managers — the one idea, the minute plan, what to say in each block, which demo and lab, when to stop, and where the material lives in this kit.

> Format name: **"Operational AI Excellence for Chapter Managers"**.
> Community format by a YPO member, not an official YPO programme.

## The one idea

> **Chairs and officers rotate every year; the Chapter Manager stays.
> AI helps them become — and keep — the memory of the chapter, without more hours.**

Every block serves this sentence. If a question pulls the room elsewhere, bring it back to it.

**"Without more hours" is a bet, not a promise.** Say it that way: *"The bet: the hours come from searching and answering twice. Test it with your own week — your log will tell you."* Do not add a number of hours or a percentage; the participants' own log is the only measure that counts.

## The three questions

Write them on the wall (a handwritten flipchart, or the first slide) before people arrive, with an empty parking-lot sheet next to them. They come from Chapter Managers themselves.

| # | Question (CM's words) | Short name | Block |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | How do I reach my members — email and WhatsApp? | **REACH** | 50–80 |
| 2 | How do I build a knowledge base, with automation where possible? | **KNOW** | 20–50 |
| 3 | How do I survive the yearly change of officers without losing what we know? | **PERSIST** | 80–106 |

We start with KNOW, not REACH: the assistant built in Lab A is the one that files mail in Lab B and writes the handover in Lab C. One build, three uses.

**Success test — every participant can show:**

1. Their assistant — the desktop app with the core and `house-rules.md` — answers a question from `chapter-facts.md`.
2. The folder structure exists in the chapter's (or for now their own) synced cloud folder.
3. One email is filed, has a log line and a reply draft, saved in the folder — not sent.

Anything not finished by the show is not a failure: it goes on the wall poster as *homework*, with the kit as the guide.

## How to read the block cards

Each block below has the same parts:

| Part | What it is |
|---|---|
| Goal | What is true in the room when the block ends |
| Talking points | Keywords, not a script. Say them in your own words |
| Role lens | Your own experience, as patterns. Fill the placeholders **before** the day. Never invent an experience — if you have none for a slot, use the pattern line from the kit and say it is a pattern you have heard |
| Demo / Lab | What you show, what they build |
| Stop mark | The minute at which you stop, wherever the room is. What is unfinished goes home with the kit |
| Kit | The files the room uses in this block |

Placeholders look like this: `[Trainer: own example as chair]` and `[Trainer: own observation as regional officer]`. Delete the ones you cannot fill honestly.

**Where you stand.** During demos you stand at the projector. During the labs you sit at a "helpdesk" table at the side of the room: people come to you, and helpers walk the room. Nothing in your hand during the show — no sheet, no list.

## Block 1 — Opening (0–10)

**Goal:** the room has named its own burning question, knows in one sentence who you are, has heard the one idea, and knows that nothing gets approved today.

**Talking points**
- **0–3 · The wall first.** Point at the three questions: *"These three questions come from you. Which one burns most today?"* Let two or three people answer. No count, no vote — just listen, and say which block answers it.
- **3–6 · Your roles, one sentence.** *"I have sat on the other side of your desk — `[Trainer: your roles in one clause, e.g. as a past chair and in a regional role]` — and I still don't know your job as well as you do."* Then one lens line each (below). In a guest room the regional role is only "in another region" — see [Running it as a guest](#running-it-as-a-guest).
- **6–10 · The one idea**, once, slowly — and then the bet: *"The hours come from searching and answering twice. Test it with your own week — your log will tell you."*
- **Greenhouse sentence:** *"Nothing gets approved today. Every 'that won't work for us' goes on the wall — that is how v0.2 gets written."* Point at the parking-lot sheet ([Parking lot](#parking-lot-and-yes-and)).
- The aim for today: a running assistant, a folder, one handled email. Not a slide deck. What does not finish here goes home with the kit.
- House rule for the room: what is said about a chapter stays in the room. We use fictional data on screens.
- No show of hands about plans, settings or the self-test. You already know the numbers from the pre-workshop form ([pre-workshop-message.md](pre-workshop-message.md)); nobody has to admit anything in front of the room.

Three worked openings, ready to say in your own cadence: [opening-variants.md](opening-variants.md) (optional).

**Role lens**
- As chair: `[Trainer: own example as chair]` — one sentence on what the Chapter Manager made possible for you.
- As regional officer: `[Trainer: own observation as regional officer]` — one sentence on what you see across chapters (pattern, no chapter names).

**Demo / Lab:** none. **Stop mark:** min 10 — even if the wall conversation is still running; it continues on the parking-lot sheet.
**Kit:** none yet; QR code to the repo on screen.

## Block 2 — Foundation (10–20)

**Goal:** everyone knows three things: the kit runs on a paid plan with training on your data switched off; the chapter's data lives in one storage the chapter owns, with at least `manager@` and `chair@` as chapter accounts; today everyone works in the desktop app — ChatGPT or Claude, either is fine — in one folder where the assistant saves its own work (Level 2 of the kit); the browser project is the fallback. Everyone who wants a lab neighbour has one.

**Talking points**
- A paid plan is the first prerequisite of this kit; training off is the one setting that makes member data workable. Show where the setting lives; details in the kit, not on your slides.
- The second prerequisite: chapter accounts in Microsoft 365 or Google Workspace for at least the Chapter Manager and the Chair (`manager@`, `chair@`) — so the handover works and the Chair can read the chapter folder; whoever has none yet takes the ready request to their Chair ([role-accounts.md](../05-officer-handover/role-accounts.md)).
- One storage, owned by the chapter — not your private Drive, not three places.
- Today: the desktop app with one folder. You open the folder, the assistant reads `house-rules.md` there and saves every file itself — no downloading, no copying. The browser project still works, it is just slower. Level 3 (Claude Code or Codex) stays optional.
- The phone: ordinary chats and drafts on the go; the folder task stays on the laptop.
- *"Who would like a neighbour in the lab?"* — the only question to the room in this block. Pair those people now with someone whose set-up works. (The pre-workshop form tells you roughly how many to expect.)
- "The chapter does not pay for your plan yet? The one-pager is the page you and your chair take to the ExCo."

**Role lens**
- As chair: `[Trainer: own example as chair]` — e.g. where chapter knowledge lived in your year, and what that cost at handover.
- As regional officer: `[Trainer: own observation as regional officer]` — e.g. where chapter knowledge most often ends up across chapters (pattern, no chapter names).
- Pattern from the kit (use if you have no own example): *three filing places* is the most common pain; *one chapter storage with a fixed structure* is the most common fix.

**Demo / Lab:** none in the room. The self-test ([../00-before-the-workshop/pre-workshop-selftest.md](../00-before-the-workshop/pre-workshop-selftest.md)) was done before the day; setting questions go to the helpdesk table during Lab A.
**Stop mark:** min 20.
**Kit:** [paid-plan-and-training-off.md](../01-foundations/paid-plan-and-training-off.md) · [where-your-data-lives.md](../01-foundations/where-your-data-lives.md) · [three-levels.md](../01-foundations/three-levels.md)

## Block 3 — KNOW (20–50) · question 2

**Goal:** every participant has a desktop task with the house rules, the chapter folder, and a short first `chapter-facts.md` (six short answers) — and has seen the assistant answer their yearly question from it. The full page grows tonight with the interview.

**Talking points**
- Today the chapter's knowledge sits in heads and private mailboxes. When an officer leaves, part of it leaves too.
- Your own files are the truth; mails and chats are sources. The assistant reads before it answers — or says UNCHECKED.
- `chapter-facts.md` becomes one page. The assistant reads it before anything else. So does a stand-in or the next officer. Today it starts with three to five lines — enough for one real answer.
- The house rules are the difference between a chatbot and a colleague. They are written for you — change them.
- Tools come and go; the files in your folder stay. The tool is replaceable, the folder is not.

**When Forum comes up** — the house rules and the one-pager keep Forum out (the full rule is the first line of the one-pager). Say it in the members' own language:
- *"AI takes the date-chasing, numbers and travel lists off your Forum work — that is time back for the people."*
- *"What is said in Forum stays there. The rule is simple: a name next to a topic is already content."*
- *"If in doubt, leave it out — it costs a minute, and Forum keeps what makes it Forum."*

**Role lens**
- As chair: `[Trainer: own example as chair]` — e.g. a question you asked your CM that only they could answer.
- As regional officer: `[Trainer: own observation as regional officer]` — e.g. the same question asked in many chapters each year.
- Pattern from the kit: *good* = a reusable run-of-show per event · *not good* = three filing places · *improved* = one chapter storage, fixed structure.

**Demo D4a (4 min, min 23–27):** ask the room first: *"How often a year do you get a bylaws question?"* Then: *"Give me one you get every year."* Type their question against the fictional bylaws excerpt. If the excerpt covers it, the answer comes with the quoted passage; if not, the assistant says UNCHECKED / [verify] — and that is the point. Then run your prepared second question, so the room sees both outcomes. Last, 45 seconds: *"Save this answer and its source as demo-answer.md in the demo folder."* Point at the file as it appears: *"You ask, the assistant saves, you check"* — the room has now seen the folder before Lab A. Script: [demo-scripts.md § D4a](demo-scripts.md#d4a--bylaws-question-know-block).

**Lab A (20 min, min 27–47)** — in this order, in the desktop app. Participant version: [lab-build-your-assistant.md](../02-your-assistant/lab-build-your-assistant.md). Everyone arrives with the app installed, the working folder `CM-Workspace` in their synced drive and the kit unzipped in `CM-Workspace/_kit` (pre-workshop check). You sit at the helpdesk table.

## Block 4 — REACH (50–80) · question 1

**Goal:** every participant has handled one email the new way: filed in the right folder, one log line, a reply draft they will send themselves. Everyone has seen what WhatsApp looks like at stage 1 — the app you build yourself (WhatsApp stage 2, Level 3) comes as a glimpse in the outlook in Block 6.

**Talking points**
- Email for what is binding, WhatsApp for what is short. Both, on purpose.
- One channel *to* the CM: officers' requests reach them through one named route, not five.
- The assistant drafts; you send. Every time, every channel. It may also draft a circular to all members — you send it yourself from Outlook, Gmail or Cvent.
- Your number is your main channel — keep it yours: read and draft with AI, send by hand — one-to-one, or one group post you send yourself. No bulk WhatsApp.
- What the chapter needs from WhatsApp — decisions, RSVPs — goes into the chapter folder as a short summary in the event file. The chat itself stays on your phone.
- Filing is the hidden win: the mail you file today is the answer someone needs next year.

**Role lens**
- As chair: `[Trainer: own example as chair]` — e.g. how requests from officers reached your CM, and what changed when they went through one route.
- As regional officer: `[Trainer: own observation as regional officer]` — e.g. what you have seen about channel discipline between officers and their CM (pattern, no chapter names).
- Pattern from the kit: *good* = a personal channel with a high read rate · *not good* = everything over the private number, officer mails not aligned · *improved* = mail for binding, WhatsApp for short, AI drafts, a human sends, a fixed weekly rhythm.
- A pattern many regions know: one CM, several officers, conflicting requests from each. This kit answers it with its own guardrails a chapter can adopt: one channel, event work budgeted separately, a briefing before officers take office — see [operating-guardrails.md](../06-chapter-rules/operating-guardrails.md). (Pattern only — no chapter, no names.)

**Demo D1 (6 min, min 54–60):** email handoff — a test mail arrives from your second test account, the assistant names the folder and file name, writes the log line, leaves a draft. You do not send. [demo-scripts.md § D1](demo-scripts.md#d1--email-handoff-reach-block)
**Demo D2 (5 min, min 60–65):** WhatsApp, stage 1 — official chat export of a fictional test chat → summary (who comes, who declines, open questions) → one-to-one drafts. The self-built app (WhatsApp stage 2) is not built here; it is part of the outlook in Block 6. [demo-scripts.md § D2](demo-scripts.md#d2--whatsapp-reach-block)

**Lab B (12 min, min 65–77):** everyone works with the same fictional Lakeside material, so the lab is paste – file – draft, not searching an inbox. Participants paste **one block** into their desktop task: the [Lab B practice pack](../02-your-assistant/lab-b-practice-pack.md) — the Lab B prompt, the fictional mail and the two bylaw sections in one copy (who is ahead takes [email-2-officer-request.md](../examples/email-2-officer-request.md) as well). Show that block on the projector; do not type your own version — the room should see exactly what the sheet says. Own mails come tonight, with the same prompt. You sit at the helpdesk table.

Then one line: `Save all three now.` The assistant saves the mail into `06-communication/inbox-filed/`, the log line at the bottom of `00-handover/log.md` and the draft into `06-communication/drafts/` — participants open the folder and see them. Browser path: they save the three texts themselves. Do not send. The draft should cite the bylaws sections and promise no seat.

**Debrief (3 min, min 77–80):** "Write down for yourself: minutes for this mail by hand, minutes with the assistant. Your mileage varies — that is why it is your number, not mine. Over a week, your log tells you whether the bet holds."
**Stop mark:** **min 77.** Who has the draft but not the log line: the log line is homework (one minute).

## Block 5 — PERSIST (80–106) · question 3

**Goal:** every participant has a first `handover.md` checkpoint and has seen someone else continue from it; knows how role addresses and a chapter-owned folder protect their work; knows what stays theirs when a contract ends; and leaves with the one-pager to take to their ExCo when it fits.

**Talking points**
- Officers change every year. The CM is the continuity — if their system is the chapter's, not theirs alone.
- Role addresses (`manager@`, `chair@` on the chapter's domain): the address stays when the person changes. Same for the cloud storage — owned by the role, two admins.
- Commitment deadlines have a calendar. Copy the current definitions from the official source into the calendar template once; the assistant reminds you every year.
- What the chapter owes its CM: one channel, lead time, a response target, fixed dates, a briefing for new officers. The rules cost no money; they ask for discipline.
- The one-pager is for the chair and ExCo to *decide* — you bring it, they adopt it.
- What stays yours: you work for your chapter, self-employed or as its employee — never for YPO Global. Your know-how, your method, your own WhatsApp number and chats stay yours; for templates and prompts built for chapter work, agree what you take with you `[check your contract]`; the chapter's data, decisions, event files and role accounts stay with the chapter — and access ends with the role. Written out, so nobody has to negotiate it at the end of a contract: [what-stays-yours.md](../05-officer-handover/what-stays-yours.md).

**Role lens**
- As chair: `[Trainer: own example as chair]` — e.g. what your handover looked like, and what you wish had been written down.
- As regional officer: `[Trainer: own observation as regional officer]` — e.g. which deadlines tend to slip around officer changes (pattern, no chapter).
- Pattern from the kit: *good* = the Past Chair stays on the ExCo · *not good* = the handover is one conversation, deadlines slip · *improved* = checklist + CM briefing for new officers + guardrails + a yearly calendar of commitments.

**Demo D4b (4 min, min 84–88):** the demo is a question, asked by the new Learning Officer in the first person: *"It's 1 July, I am the new Learning Officer. What is open, what does done look like — and who is confirmed as speaker for the Spring Retreat?"* The brief comes from the fictional folder with sources; the last answer is UNCHECKED, because no file says it. That gap is the lesson. [demo-scripts.md § D4b](demo-scripts.md#d4b--handover-brief-persist-block)

**Take-away walk-through (5 min, min 88–93):** role addresses ([role-accounts.md](../05-officer-handover/role-accounts.md)) · the chapter folder · what stays yours ([what-stays-yours.md](../05-officer-handover/what-stays-yours.md)) · the one-pager ([ai-use-one-pager.md](../06-chapter-rules/ai-use-one-pager.md)) and the guardrails ([operating-guardrails.md](../06-chapter-rules/operating-guardrails.md)). On the ExCo: *"If it fits, write on your copy which ExCo meeting you would bring it to."* Private, on their copy — no question to the room.

**Lab C (4 min, min 93–97):** paste the checkpoint prompt into the desktop task:

```
Checkpoint now. Refresh 00-handover/handover.md: what is set up, open items (each with what "done"
looks like), paths touched. Then stop.
```

The assistant saves `00-handover/handover.md` itself (browser path: participants save it). Fictional example: [../examples/handover.md](../examples/handover.md).
**Stop mark:** **min 97** — whatever is in the file now is what the new officer gets.

**"Chairs rotate" (6 min, min 97–103) — this moment is never cut.**

Before anyone stands up (inside minute 1):
- The Lab C checkpoint prompt has started on every laptop — the switch waits for it, not the other way round.
- Say the one sentence that must not fall away with the walk-through: *"The facts belong to the chapter; the way you keep them is yours ([what-stays-yours.md](../05-officer-handover/what-stays-yours.md)) — and the one-pager is the page you and your chair take to the ExCo."*
- The announcement: *"You stay with the laptop. The person who moves is the new officer on 1 July — ask only what these files can answer."*

| Min | What happens |
|---|---|
| 1 | Two groups at every table: A stays seated, B stands up, takes their laptop and moves one chair along. B now sits next to a different person. Projector: *"It's 1 July. You are the new Learning Officer."* |
| 2½ | A starts a **new** task with `CM-Workspace` — the memory is the folder, not the chat — and types the prompt below with B's two questions from the projector: *"What is open right now, and what does done look like?"* and *"Who do I ask for {{something B needs — e.g. the venue contract}}?"* A types the questions into their assistant; both read the answer from `chapter-facts.md` and `handover.md`. |
| 2½ | Swap: A asks B the same two questions, on B's laptop. |

The restart prompt A types — it loads the house rules from the chapter folder, so nobody pastes the core again (B's second question goes in at the end):

```
Read house-rules.md in the folder ending in -chapter here — not the one in _kit — and follow it. Then, from that same chapter folder, read 00-handover/handover.md and 02-chapter-knowledge/chapter-facts.md and answer only from them: 1) What is open right now, and what does done look like? 2) Who do I ask for <B's question>?
```

Projector during the round: the two questions only — nothing else. A question the file cannot answer = UNCHECKED = homework, not a failure.

Fallbacks: odd number of people → you move too and play the new officer. A file not finished → use the fictional Lakeside files ([../examples/handover.md](../examples/handover.md), [../examples/chapter-facts.md](../examples/chapter-facts.md)) and say it openly: *"This shows the method, not your chapter."* Short of time → cut the outlook, then the walk-through — not this.

**Debrief (3 min, min 103–106):** two questions, in this order.
1. *"Could someone continue tomorrow from this file?"* — a few answers; every gap someone found is tonight's homework.
2. *"What in this file could only you have written? That is your method — it goes with you."*

## Block 6 — Show & outlook (106–115)

**Goal:** each participant has shown their three results to a partner; the wall poster shows, without names, how many results run and how many go home as homework; everyone has had a glimpse of what comes next.

**Show in pairs** (6 min, min 106–112): pairs, all at the same time, three minutes each way — (1) the project answering from `chapter-facts.md`, (2) the folder, (3) the filed mail + log line + draft. Nobody presents to the room. You walk around and listen, with nothing in your hand.

**Self-count** (2 min, min 112–114): the wall poster from [harvest.md](harvest.md) has one row per result and two columns, *running* and *homework*. Everyone puts three dots — one per row — and one more dot in the last row if all three run. You do not count at the seats and you do not ask for hands. Homework is a fine answer: it means the step is clear and the kit has it. Who wants to can add a sticky note: *"What is the kit missing?"*

**Outlook** (1 min, min 114–115) — one 60-second video, no live demo:
- Your own WhatsApp app (D2 stage 2): an app the Chapter Manager built with their AI tool reads a chat, writes a summary and reply drafts into the folder.
- Browser agent (D3): an agent reads the next three events from a **mock** member portal into a table.
- Canva: text from the event file, design from Canva.
- Details for all three are in the kit — nobody needs them this month. Scripts and set-up: [demo-scripts.md § Outlook](demo-scripts.md#outlook--three-glimpses-in-one-minute).

**Role lens:** none needed — let the participants be the experts here.
**Stop mark:** **min 115.** Cut the outlook before you cut the pairs.
**Kit:** [email-connectors.md](../07-connectors-and-browser/email-connectors.md) · [canva.md](../07-connectors-and-browser/canva.md) · [browser-agent.md](../07-connectors-and-browser/browser-agent.md)

## Block 7 — Close (115–120)

**Goal:** everyone knows their next step, has the repo, and has given feedback.

**Talking points**
- The bet, once more: *"The hours come from searching and answering twice. Test it with your own week — your log will tell you."* START-HERE has the path for the first weeks; the first month takes more time than the ones after.
- The QR code to the repo. Everything you saw is in it, including the demos.
- Follow-up in two weeks: `[Trainer: date and format, e.g. 30-minute online drop-in]`. All messages after today come through one channel agreed with the organiser — see [follow-up.md](follow-up.md).
- Three feedback questions + "what should go into the kit" ([harvest.md](harvest.md)). Anonymous. Say where it goes: to the region as patterns only — in a guest room, to the host.
- Last sentence: the one idea, once more.

**Stop mark:** min 120 — on time. CMs plan their day around it.

## Your two roles — speaking from both without talking down

The format works best with a trainer who has been a chair (or past chair) and holds a regional role. That combination is also its risk.

**The ground truth:** a Chapter Manager works *for the chapter* — self-employed or employed by it (a small association, sometimes a small company) — not for you, not for the region and never for YPO Global. That is why the chapter's own AI rules matter. You are a guest in their professional world.

| Do | Instead of |
|---|---|
| "I have sat on both sides — this is what I saw." | "This is how you should do it." |
| "As chair, I made their job harder when I …" `[Trainer: own example as chair]` | Stories where the CM is the problem |
| "Across chapters I see a pattern …" | Comparing chapters or CMs in the room |
| "What does your chapter owe you? Here is a page you can take to your chair." | "Your board should …" |
| Ask the room first, then add your view | Opening with your solution |
| Name what chairs get wrong | Naming what CMs get wrong |

**When you are not a chair or regional officer.** An experienced CM can run this format from their own role: replace the two lenses with `[Trainer: own example as Chapter Manager]` and, if possible, invite a chair for five minutes in Block 5. See [run-it-yourself.md](run-it-yourself.md).

## Running it as a guest

When another region invites you — typically after the host's own session in the same afternoon — you have no role towards anyone in the room. Keep it that way.

| Point | How |
|---|---|
| Handover | The host introduces you and hands over in one sentence, e.g. *"{{HOST}} invited me. I bring a kit; you decide what stays."* Agree the sentence with the host beforehand |
| Your roles | Past chair first. The regional role only as "a regional officer in another region" — one clause, no more |
| Hands | None. The host's session may already have asked the room things; do not repeat that. Plan and self-test status come from the pre-workshop form |
| Optional, guest rooms only: chair count (2 min, inside 0–3) | *"Keep your hand up if you have worked with two chairs … three … five …"* Add it up out loud as one sum: *"In this room: N chair-years — and you were there for all of them."* Only the sum, never who. The wall question shrinks to one minute |
| Harvest | Goes to the host, and you say so: *"The anonymous sheet goes to {{HOST}}, not to me and not to my region."* Send the note to the host; the host decides what goes further ([harvest.md](harvest.md)) |
| Follow-up | Through the host's channel, agreed beforehand ([follow-up.md](follow-up.md)) |
| Examples | Nothing from your own region by name; patterns only |

## Running it in your own region

When you hold a regional role for some of the chapters in the room, your role reports on chapters — not on Chapter Managers. People will still wonder what reaches their chair. Design the room so that question never comes up.

| Point | How |
|---|---|
| Room | Tables of 3–4, no lectern. You stand only during the demos; during the labs you sit at the helpdesk table |
| First sentence | *"These three questions come from you. Which one burns most today?"* — before anything about yourself |
| One sentence on your role | *"Nothing I hear today goes back to any chapter with a name on it. What I take to the region is patterns and the feedback sheet — anonymous."* |
| No checks in public | No hands on paid plan, setting or self-test — collected quietly beforehand with the pre-workshop form. No chair count |
| No tally by you | Self-count with dots on the wall poster; you walk the show with nothing in your hand |
| The ExCo | Private: *"write on your copy when it fits"* — never a question to the room |
| Language | Moderate in the room's language; material stays English ([Language variants](#language-variants)) |

Then keep it:

- Do not use what you hear for any assessment of a chapter or a CM.
- If a CM describes a problem with their board, acknowledge it, point to the guardrails page as something they can bring themselves, and do not take it up on their behalf unless they ask you to — after the session, one to one.
- Avoid examples from chapters that are in the room, even anonymised.
- In the harvest, report counts and themes, never a person or a chapter ([harvest.md](harvest.md)).

## 90-minute version

For a shorter slot. The cut: Foundation shrinks to one slide, D2 runs as a short video, the outlook goes (one sentence in the close points to the kit), the walk-through shrinks to a pointer, debriefs shrink. Lab A stays whole, because it is the success test, and "Chairs rotate" stays whole, because it is the moment people remember. The minutes in each row add up to the row; the rows add up to exactly 90.

| Min | Block | What happens (minutes) |
|---|---|---|
| 0–8 | Opening | The wall: "Which of these three burns most today?" (2 min) · two roles in one sentence + lens (2 min) · the one idea as a bet, greenhouse sentence, room rule (4 min) |
| 8–12 | Foundation | One slide: paid plan + training off, one storage, today in the desktop app (3 min) · "Who would like a neighbour in the lab?" (1 min) |
| 12–37 | KNOW | Talking points + lens (2 min) · D4a with one question from the room (3 min) · **Lab A unchanged** — desktop task + core, the assistant builds the folder and saves a short `chapter-facts.md` (20 min); the pre-workshop check is a must here. Stop mark min 37; the interview is homework |
| 37–59 | REACH | Talking points + lens (2 min) · D1 live (5 min) · D2 stage 1 as 90-second backup video (2 min) · **Lab B** with the fictional Lakeside mail (12 min) · debrief, one question (1 min). Stop mark min 58 |
| 59–80 | PERSIST | Lens + talking points incl. what stays yours (2 min) · D4b, the new Learning Officer asks (3 min) · walk-through as a pointer: role addresses, [what-stays-yours.md](../05-officer-handover/what-stays-yours.md), one-pager (3 min) · **Lab C** checkpoint (4 min), stop mark min 71 · **"Chairs rotate"** unchanged (6 min) · debrief, both questions (3 min) |

If you have even less time, cut demos before labs and Lab B before Lab A. Without Lab A nobody leaves with something running; without "Chairs rotate" nobody has seen why it matters.

## 105-minute version with a coffee break

For a slot of 2 × 45–60 minutes with a break in between (for example 45 min · 15 min coffee · 60 min). It is the 90-minute version plus two buffers — no new content. The break comes right after Lab A, so the coffee break is also your helpdesk for the laptops that have no folder yet.

| Min | Block | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 0–37 | Opening · Foundation · KNOW | As in the 90-minute version, rows 0–8, 8–12 and 12–37. **Lab A starts with the unzip, all together (3 min):** `_kit.zip` from Downloads into `CM-Workspace`, the right folder picture on the projector, rename `_kit (1)` or `_kit 2` to `_kit`; on a Mac with Safari there may be no ZIP at all — Safari unpacks it by itself, so a folder `_kit` already sits in Downloads: move that folder into `CM-Workspace`. No installs after minute 10: whoever has no desktop app sits next to a neighbour and takes the *Browser path* |
| 37–45 | Buffer | Check: the short `chapter-facts.md` is in the folder. Whoever is stuck stays on the browser path next to their neighbour |
| Break | Coffee, 15 min | You walk to the open laptops only |
| 45–98 | REACH · PERSIST · Show · Close | As in the 90-minute version, rows 37–90 (minus 8) |

**Must-haves of the day, for every participant on her own laptop:** `CM-Workspace/_kit` in place and one saved draft from the Lakeside mail (Lab B) in her own folder. No paid plan yet? She works next to her neighbour, saves the shared Lakeside draft as a file in her own `CM-Workspace`, and sets up her plan that evening. **Check at the end of Lab B (1 min, never cut):** each participant opens her own folder and the draft file in it. **If time is lost, cut in this order:** Show in pairs (5) · close down to two sentences (3) · the PERSIST walk-through pointer (3) · debriefs to one question each (3). ## Language variants

| Situation | How |
|---|---|
| Material | English throughout — so it travels across YPO Europe |
| Moderation | In the room's language. A German-speaking room can be moderated in German with English slides and files |
| Mixed room | Speak the majority language; keep prompts and file names in English so everyone can copy them |
| Prompts | Work in any language. The house rules already say: *write in the language of the recipient* |
| Assistant replies | Participants can add "Answer me in German" (or any language) to the house rules |

Key terms for German moderation:

| English (in the kit) | German (spoken) |
|---|---|
| house rules | Hausregeln |
| truth / sources | Wahrheit / Quellen |
| draft, never send | entwerfen, nie senden |
| handover / checkpoint | Übergabe / Zwischenstand |
| role address | Rollen-Adresse |
| filed / log line | abgelegt / Logzeile |
| one channel | ein Kanal |
