# Business plan checklist — pick, compare, check

Purpose: choose the AI plan the chapter buys, compare the big providers on one page, and check the details before anyone pays.
Time: 2 minutes to pick, 15 minutes to check, then 10 minutes a quarter.

**Facts on this page: as of 21 Oct 2026, from the providers' own pages — every fact links to its source.**
Prices are US list prices in USD per seat, before tax; your checkout in euros or pounds can differ.
*"Not stated by the vendor — ask"* means we could not find it on the provider's own page: ask their sales or support before you buy.
Plans change fast — that is why this page ends with a prompt that lets your AI re-check it.

## A paid plan is required. A business plan is ideal.

Chapter accounts in Microsoft 365 or Google Workspace — at least `manager@` and `chair@` — are required too; the table below builds on them.

- **No training by default** — business plans keep chapter chats out of model training without a switch to remember.
- **A data processing agreement** — a contract for the member data the chapter works with, with the provider as processor.
- **Admin** — the chapter owns the workspace and adds or removes people when roles change.
- **Connectors** — mail and file connections are switched on once, centrally, for everyone in the workspace.

## Pick in 2 minutes

The chapter needs accounts in **Microsoft 365 or Google Workspace** anyway — at least `manager@` for the Chapter Manager and `chair@` for the Chair,
so the handover works and the Chair can read the chapter folder ([`../05-officer-handover/role-accounts.md`](../05-officer-handover/role-accounts.md)).
That makes the natural pick the AI on those same accounts: one admin, one bill, one place for mail, files and AI.

## Grok Bot — what it is today

**What it is.** AI teammates on one persistent cloud computer. You message them in an app, give them a job, and they work with apps,
files and routines. A Team Bot is one shared bot for a team ([get started](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/get-started) · [Team Bots](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/team-bots)).

**Where it runs.** Desktop apps for macOS, Windows and Linux; apps for iPhone and Android. Team Bots also work in **Slack** — direct messages and channels.
Mail and Slack messages come to you as an **editable draft, and you press Send** — the kit's "a human sends" rule built in. A bot can have its own email address;
its outgoing mail waits as a draft too ([mobile](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/mobile) · [chat](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/chat-and-collaboration) · [changelog](https://x.ai/changelog/bot)).
WhatsApp and Telegram: not stated by the vendor — ask.

**Which plan.** Grok Bot comes with **The X Subscription, Premium or higher** (Premium, Super, Ultra — not Plus). Premium for one person: $30 a month or $300 a year.
Team seats: Premium $30, Super $100, Ultra $200 a month; a yearly team price is not stated ([pricing](https://docs.x.ai/x-subscription/pricing) · [Grok Bot on X Subscription](https://docs.x.ai/x-subscription/grok-bot)).
The Grok Business page does not list Grok Bot — ask whether your Business seats include it.

**Your data.** With team Privacy Mode on, your data is not used for training. Grok Bot runs only on hosted cloud computers that keep files and browser sessions.
There is no separate zero-retention control in Grok Bot and no per-organisation retention policy ([security](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/security)).
So: use it for chapter work without member data — event research, venue requests, newsletter outlines — and keep member data out until the retention question is answered in writing.

## Five settings worth checking

It starts before AI: two-factor sign-in on your email, your cloud storage and your AI account, and a clear picture of the tools you already use — what each one stores, where, and who can see it.
Then, in a chapter-managed pro or business account, check the five areas YPO's AI guidance for Chapter Managers (October 2026) names:

| Area | What to check |
|---|---|
| Training off | For the whole account, not only your own settings: prompts, files, outputs and usage data do not train the provider's models |
| Secure sign-in | Two-factor sign-in (MFA); single sign-on or managed sign-in where your plan has it |
| Sharing | Few admins and users; public links and external sharing off unless you need them |
| Connected apps | Review connectors, plug-ins, APIs and data sources now and then; remove connections you did not approve or no longer use |
| Usage and updates | Activity log, usage limits and alerts where the plan offers them; automatic updates on |

These are checks, not a gate. YPO's guidance adds: a business or pro licence does not by itself make YPO Data fine to use — see [YPO Data or chapter data?](where-your-data-lives.md#ypo-data-or-chapter-data)

# Where your data lives

Purpose: decide on one storage for all chapter work — owned by the chapter, readable by your AI, and synced to your laptop once you move to Level 2.
Time: 15 minutes to decide, one conversation with your Chair to make it official.

## The idea in one line

**One storage. The chapter owns it. You run it. Your AI reads it. Whoever steps in can continue from it.**

## Four rules

| Rule | In practice |
|---|---|
| **One storage** | Google Drive *or* OneDrive — not both, not three. Mail attachments, summaries of WhatsApp chats and downloads end up in the folder, not on the desktop |
| **The chapter owns it** | The chapter has accounts in Microsoft 365 or Google Workspace — required: at least `manager@<chapter-domain>` for you and `chair@<chapter-domain>` for your Chair. The storage belongs to `manager@`, and `chair@` can read it, so the handover works and your Chair sees the chapter folder. Chair and Chapter Manager are both admins; the Past Chair is the reserve. See [`../05-officer-handover/role-accounts.md`](../05-officer-handover/role-accounts.md) |
| **Synced to your laptop — from Level 2** | Level 1 needs no sync app: you work in the browser and upload files. From Level 2, install the storage's desktop app so the same folder lives on your laptop; Level 2 and Level 3 tools work in that local copy, the cloud keeps the chapter's copy |
| **One structure** | The ten folders from [`../02-your-assistant/folder-structure.md`](../02-your-assistant/folder-structure.md), the same in every chapter that uses this kit |

## Google world or Microsoft world?

The chapter accounts live in one of the two. Pick the world your chapter already lives in — both work.

| | Google world | Microsoft world |
|---|---|---|
| Storage | Google Drive (a shared drive owned by the chapter account is ideal) `[verify plan]` | OneDrive or a SharePoint site owned by the chapter account `[verify plan]` |
| Mail | Gmail on the chapter domain | Outlook on the chapter domain |
| AI inside the suite | Gemini Gems read files from Drive, always the current version, once Google Workspace is connected to Gemini and activity is on ([source](https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/15146780)) · Gemini in Workspace: no training without permission ([source](https://support.google.com/a/answer/15706919)) — so on the chapter accounts activity stays on; the "Keep Activity → off" switch in [paid-plan-and-training-off.md](paid-plan-and-training-off.md) is for personal Google accounts | Copilot Notebooks take up to 300 files and a SharePoint or OneDrive folder as a source; also on Microsoft 365 Personal, Family and Premium · Work accounts: Enterprise Data Protection ([source](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/copilot/microsoft-365/enterprise-data-protection)) |
| Claude connector | Gmail, Calendar, Drive — all users ([source](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/10166901)) | Microsoft 365 — work accounts only, one-time admin consent; on Team and Enterprise the Claude owner enables it and an admin switches on write tools ([source](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/15183774)) |

As of 21 Oct 2026. Details per connector: [`../07-connectors-and-browser/email-connectors.md`](../07-connectors-and-browser/email-connectors.md).

**Bonus:** the chapter account `manager@` is also what the Microsoft connector needs — a *work* account.
One decision solves two problems.

## Why Markdown files, not a database

Your knowledge base is a folder of short text files (`.md`). Every AI reads them well, they open on any computer,
they sync like any other file, and the next Chapter Manager needs no special software. A database would need someone to run it.

## What goes in, what stays out

| Goes in | Stays out — and where it goes instead |
|---|---|
| Bylaws, guidelines, event files, budgets, invoices, filed mails, summaries and decisions from WhatsApp chats, reports, minutes | **Forum content** → stays in the Forum. Forum is sacred. We use AI for Forum logistics only: dates, places, headcounts, costs, deadlines. Never for what is said in or around a Forum: topics, stories, notes, retreat conversations, anything shared in confidence, or any link between a name and a topic. If in doubt, leave it out. |
| Officer names, roles and role addresses; members' email, mobile number and the business details you need for invoicing; who came to which event | **Passwords, PINs, card numbers** → the chapter's password manager |
| Prospect pipeline, onboarding steps | **A copy of the member directory** → link to YPO Connect instead |
| Your learnings after each event | **Home addresses, ID or passport copies, health or family details** → not needed for the role |

## YPO Data or chapter data?

YPO's AI guidance for Chapter Managers (October 2026) draws one line that is useful to know. It is guidance, not a rule today.

| | What it is | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **YPO Data** | What comes from YPO's systems — the guidance names Salesforce, Cvent and the Chapter Management Hub; you usually open them as YPO Connect and the Hub. Exports, copies and anything derived from them stay YPO Data | Member directory, YPO Global event lists, membership reports, exports from YPO systems |
| **Chapter data** | What your chapter collects itself, not taken or derived from YPO Data | Your own surveys and sign-ups, chapter event engagement, mails and files that did not come from a YPO system |

Chapter data is yours to work with; Forum content stays out either way. For YPO Data, YPO has proposed an AI appendix to its Operations Manual for 2027, subject to its Board's approval. If the Board approves it, connecting AI to YPO systems or sharing YPO Data with an AI tool would need YPO's approval.
Until then you can work as this kit describes — and look at the policy again when it comes. Questions: your Regional Manager or aigovernance@ypo.org.

Purpose: one folder structure for the whole chapter, so you, your assistant, a stand-in and every new officer always know where a thing lives.
Copy [`template-folder/`](template-folder/) into the chapter's cloud storage. That is the whole setup. Already have a structure that works? Keep it — see [*Already have a folder structure that works?*](#already-have-a-folder-structure-that-works) below.

## The idea in one line

**The folder belongs to the chapter, not to you.** You run it. Your assistant keeps it true. A stand-in or the next officer can continue from it.
What stays yours — your know-how, your own templates and method, your own WhatsApp number and chats — is set out in [`../05-officer-handover/what-stays-yours.md`](../05-officer-handover/what-stays-yours.md).
Your hours log lives outside the chapter folder, in your own folder — see [`../10-time-by-area/`](../10-time-by-area/).

## Where it lives and who owns it

| Point | How |
| --- | --- |
| One storage | One cloud storage for all chapter work — Google Drive or OneDrive. Not three. See [`../01-foundations/where-your-data-lives.md`](../01-foundations/where-your-data-lives.md) |
| Owner | An account that belongs to the **role**, in the chapter's Microsoft 365 or Google Workspace — required: at least `manager@<chapter-domain>` and `chair@<chapter-domain>`. The folder belongs to `manager@` and `chair@` can open it, so it passes on with the role. See [`../05-officer-handover/role-accounts.md`](../05-officer-handover/role-accounts.md) |
| Two admins | The Chapter Chair and the Chapter Manager both have full rights. The Past Chair is the reserve. Nobody is the only key |
| On your laptop | Level 1 needs no sync app — you upload files to your AI project. From Level 2 on, install the storage's desktop sync app, so the same folder is also on your laptop; Level 2 and Level 3 tools work in that local copy — see [`../01-foundations/three-levels.md`](../01-foundations/three-levels.md) |
| Sharing | Share sub-folders with officers when they need them (for example `03-events` with the Learning Officer). Keep `05-finance` to the Chair, the finance officer and you |

**Project files are copies.** Files you upload to a ChatGPT or Claude project do not follow changes in your folder — after a change you upload again. Keep the project small (`house-rules.md`, `chapter-facts.md`, the bylaws). Where the desktop app has access to your folder, do the daily work there — nothing to upload. Without folder access, upload a changed file again the same day and ask the assistant which version it has.

Every prompt in this kit works the same with OneDrive: where a prompt says "Drive", read your cloud storage — Google Drive or OneDrive.

No chapter accounts yet? Ask your Chair for `manager@` and `chair@`, and meanwhile start in your own Drive or OneDrive — the structure moves into `manager@` in one step once it exists.

| `09-archive` | Closed years (`FY25-26/`) and finished events, moved here whole | Nothing gets deleted to get here — move, never delete |

Two things never go anywhere in the folder: **passwords** and **Forum content**.
Passwords have a better place: the chapter's password manager. For Forum, one rule:

> Forum is sacred. We use AI for Forum logistics only: dates, places, headcounts, costs, deadlines. Never for what is said in or around a Forum: topics, stories, notes, retreat conversations, anything shared in confidence, or any link between a name and a topic. If in doubt, leave it out.

Forum logistics belong in the folder like any other logistics: Forum dates, training registrations, retreat travel and costs.
Forum content stays in the Forum: a retreat agenda with topics or exercises, a moderator's mail about a topic, parking-lot sheets, flipcharts, notes and minutes.

## File names

`YYYY-MM-DD_topic.md` — date first, lower case, hyphens, no spaces, and **always with the extension**: `.md` for text, `.csv` for tables, the original's own for PDFs and photos.

| Kind | Example |
| --- | --- |
| Note or document | `2026-11-12_venue-options.md` |
| Filed email | `2026-11-12_learning-officer_speaker-confirmation.md` (`date_sender-role_topic.md`) |
| Event folder | `2026-12-04_holiday-dinner/` |
| Export | `2026-10-01_commitments-status-export.pdf` |
| WhatsApp summary (in the event folder) | `2026-11-14_whatsapp-summary.md` |

Roles in file names, not member names. The date sorts the folder for you. Living files keep a plain name: `chapter-facts.md`, `FAQ.md`, `handover.md`, `log.md`, `decisions.md`.

It lives at `02-chapter-knowledge/chapter-facts.md`. A finished fictional example: [`../examples/chapter-facts.md`](../examples/chapter-facts.md).

## Step 1 — Paste this prompt into your assistant project

```
Interview me to build chapter-facts.md for my chapter.
Rules for the interview:
- Ask ONE question at a time, in the order below. Wait for my answer.
- Keep each question short. Give one example answer so I know the level of detail.
- If I say "skip" or "don't know", write [verify] in that field and move on.
- Roles before names. For officers, record role, name, role email address, term and status (confirmed, provisional or [verify]) only.
- Already have a chapter-facts.md without a Status column? Add it to the officer table, every row [verify], and ask me row by row.
  No private phone numbers, no home addresses, no family details.
- Do not invent anything. If my answer is unclear, ask once, then write [verify].

The 8 questions:
1. Chapter: the chapter's name, city or area, and roughly how many members.
2. Chapter year: when the term starts and ends, and when officers change.
3. Officers: each officer role, who holds it now, who is elect for the next term (and from when), their role email address, their term, and whether the choice is confirmed or still provisional. Provisional means the chapter has not finished the decision.
4. Standard events: the events that come back every year, with month and a one-line description.
5. Budget lines: the categories your chapter budget is split into (for example learning events, speakers, Forum, administration), who approves spending, and where the budget file lives.
6. Channels: how members hear from the chapter (email, WhatsApp, other), and the ONE channel officers use to reach me.
7. Deadlines: fixed dates in the year (commitments, elections, reports, renewals) and who owns each.
8. Templates and sources: where templates, logos, past event files and the bylaws live today.

- Write chapter-facts.md in the template format below, filled with my answers.
- Put "Last checked: <today's date>" at the top.
- List every [verify] field at the end under "Open points", each with who could confirm it.
- If you can save files, save it to 02-chapter-knowledge/chapter-facts.md and add one line to 00-handover/log.md.
  If you cannot, show me the full text and the path, and I save it.

Template format:
{{PASTE THE TEMPLATE BELOW}}
```

# Role accounts — mail and files that belong to the chapter

**In one sentence:** the chapter's mail and files live in accounts owned by the *role* — manager@, chair@ — and in
one cloud folder owned by the *chapter*, so that every handover is a password reset instead of a treasure hunt.

**Required by this kit:** the chapter has Microsoft 365 or Google Workspace with **at least two accounts — `manager@` for the
Chapter Manager and `chair@` for the Chair.** That is what makes the handover work and lets the Chair read the chapter folder.

## Why this matters to you

- **Officers change every year, you stay.** When a Chair leaves, the mail threads with venues, speakers and sponsors
  should stay with the chapter. With chair@ they do.
- **You work for the chapter — self-employed or as its employee.** Your own mailbox, your WhatsApp number and your
  know-how are yours. Chapter work in a chapter account keeps that line clean — for you and for the chapter
  ([what-stays-yours.md](what-stays-yours.md)).
- **A stand-in or the next officer can continue,** from a working system instead of an empty inbox. That is the most generous handover there is.
- **Your Chair can read the chapter folder** with chair@ in the same workspace — no forwarded attachments, no "can you send me that again".
- **Your assistant works better** when everything it should know sits in one place the chapter owns.

## Who owns what

| Thing | Owner | Who uses it | Admins |
|---|---|---|---|
| manager@{{chapter-domain}} — **required** | The chapter | The Chapter Manager in office | Chair + CM |
| chair@{{chapter-domain}} — **required** | The chapter | The Chair in office | Chair + CM |
| events@{{chapter-domain}} *(optional)* | The chapter | Whoever runs registrations and venues | Chair + CM |
| Chapter cloud folder (Google Drive or OneDrive) | The chapter, held by manager@ — not a personal account | CM and Chair, officers as needed | Chair + CM |
| Password manager / vault for chapter logins | The chapter | CM, Chair | Chair + CM |
| Your own phone number and WhatsApp chats, your own mail, your general templates, a personal AI plan if you have one | You | You | You |

**WhatsApp stays on your own number.** Role accounts cover mail and files — there is no chapter WhatsApp number to set up.
What the chapter needs from a chat (decisions, RSVPs, promises) goes into the chapter folder as a short summary:
export the chat, let your assistant summarise it, file the summary in the event or member file
([../03-communication/whatsapp.md](../03-communication/whatsapp.md)). The chat itself stays on your phone.

**Two admins, always.** Chair and CM are both admins of the role accounts and the chapter folder. The Past Chair can be
a reserve admin for a year. If one person is unreachable, nothing is locked.

## How to get there

### The requirement: Microsoft 365 or Google Workspace, at least manager@ and chair@

The chapter has (or registers) its own domain and runs a small Google Workspace or Microsoft 365 setup on it,
with **at least two accounts: manager@ and chair@**. events@ and more only if useful. The shared chapter folder lives there too.

- Handover = the admin resets the password and the new person signs in. Mail history, folder, calendar stay.
- Your assistant can connect to one chapter mailbox and one chapter folder
  (see [../07-connectors-and-browser/email-connectors.md](../07-connectors-and-browser/email-connectors.md)).
- Plans, prices and any association or nonprofit offers change — check them on the day you set it up **[verify]**.
  Who pays is an ExCo decision.

Some chapters already have a domain or a workspace. Ask your Chair and the previous CM first — and your Regional Manager
whether there is a recommendation for chapter accounts in your region **[verify]**.

### An extra, not a replacement: forwarding addresses

Further role addresses — events@, info@ — can simply **forward** to manager@ or to the person in the role.

- The address on the website, on invitations and in YPO tools stays the same. When the person changes,
  you change the forwarding — members keep writing to the same address.
- File chapter mails into the chapter folder (`06-communication/inbox-filed/`) — the email handoff in
  [../03-communication/email-handoff.md](../03-communication/email-handoff.md) does exactly that.

Forwarding fixes an address. It keeps no history and gives no one the chapter folder — which is why manager@ and chair@ are real accounts.

### Until the accounts exist

Start today in your own Google Drive or OneDrive with the kit's folder structure, and put "chapter accounts manager@ + chair@,
then move the folder" in `00-handover/handover.md`. When manager@ exists, the whole folder moves across in one step
([../01-foundations/where-your-data-lives.md](../01-foundations/where-your-data-lives.md)).

### What we leave behind: chapter work only in a private mailbox

Chapter work that lives only in someone's private mailbox and private drive leaves with that person.
Moving it into a role account or the chapter folder is the whole point of this page.

## How to propose this to your Chair — a conversation guide

You do not need to ask for anything for yourself. You are offering the chapter a way to keep its own memory.
The Chair decides; you make the decision easy.

**1. Pick the moment.** Good moments: while next year's officers are being chosen, before the handover to a new Chair,
or at the first ExCo of the year. A 15-minute slot is enough.

**2. Open with the chapter's benefit, not your workload.**
> "Every year we lose a bit of the chapter's history when officers change. I'd like to suggest a simple way to keep it —
> it takes one decision from the ExCo."

**3. Show the picture in one sentence.**
> "Chapter mail goes to manager@ and chair@ — two accounts in Microsoft 365 or Google Workspace on a domain the chapter owns —
> and our files live in one chapter folder you can open too.
> When someone hands over, the admin resets a password — and everything is still there."

**4. Name what you will do.**
> "I can prepare the setup, the folder structure and the handover notes. From you I'd need three decisions:
> Microsoft 365 or Google Workspace, who are the two admins, and by when."

**5. Bring the one page.** Hand over the AI-use one-pager from
[../06-chapter-rules/ai-use-one-pager.md](../06-chapter-rules/ai-use-one-pager.md) — rule 4 is exactly this point, ready for the ExCo to adopt.

**6. Keep the start small.**
> "Two accounts are enough to start — manager@ and chair@. events@ and anything else can follow when we want them."

**7. Follow up in writing the same day.** Ask your assistant:

```
Draft a short follow-up email to {{CHAIR_ROLE}} after today's conversation about role accounts.
Summarise: the proposal (chapter accounts manager@ and chair@ in Microsoft 365 or Google Workspace, one chapter folder, two admins),
the three decisions we need (Microsoft 365 or Google Workspace, the two admins, the date), and what I offered to prepare.
Use only what is in 00-handover/handover.md and my notes below; mark anything else [verify]. I send it myself.
---
{{YOUR_NOTES}}
```

Then add one line to `00-handover/log.md`: date · role accounts · conversation with Chair · what was decided · next step.

## Moving the folder into manager@

Started on the bridge — the chapter folder in your own drive? Once `manager@` exists, it moves in one step (menu names as of Oct 2026 `[verify]`):

- **OneDrive:** in your own OneDrive on the web, select the chapter folder → Download (it arrives as one ZIP) → unzip it (Windows: right-click → Extract all · Mac: double-click) → sign in as `manager@` at office.com → OneDrive → Upload → Folder → pick the unzipped folder.
- **Google Drive:** in your own Drive, right-click the chapter folder → Download (one ZIP) → unzip it → sign in as `manager@` → Drive → New → Folder upload → pick the unzipped folder.
- Then: check that the ten folders are there, share the folder with `chair@`, note the new place in `00-handover/setup-progress.md`, and remove the bridge copy so there is one storage.

## When it is set up

- [ ] At least two accounts in Microsoft 365 or Google Workspace: manager@ (Chapter Manager) and chair@ (Chair)
- [ ] Role addresses listed in `02-chapter-knowledge/chapter-facts.md`
- [ ] Two admins named in `01-role-and-calendar/` (and in the ExCo minutes)
- [ ] Chapter folder owned by manager@, chair@ can open it, shared with the people who need it
- [ ] Chapter logins in the chapter's password manager, not in chats or files
- [ ] Role addresses entered wherever members and partners find the chapter (website, invitations, YPO tools where you manage them)
- [ ] Handover steps updated in [handover-checklist.md](handover-checklist.md)
- [ ] Agreed that access ends with the role: when someone leaves a role, the admins remove them the same day
