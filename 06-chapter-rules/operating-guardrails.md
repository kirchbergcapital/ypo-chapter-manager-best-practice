# Operating guardrails — how our ExCo and our Chapter Manager work together

## The text for the ExCo

> **{{CHAPTER}} · Operating guardrails · Proposed by the Chair for ExCo decision · {{DATE}}**
>
> Our Chapter Manager works for the chapter — self-employed or as its employee — and for the whole ExCo. To make that work well for everyone, we agree on five simple rules
> for FY {{YEAR}}.
>
> 1. **One channel.** All requests to the Chapter Manager go via the Chair or a named person ({{NAME_OR_ROLE}}).
>    The Chapter Manager receives one prioritised list and works from it.
> 2. **Lead time.** Officer updates and material reach the Chapter Manager one week before the date they are needed.
> 3. **Response target.** The Chapter Manager answers within 24 hours or gives a date. The ExCo does the same when
>    the Chapter Manager asks for a decision.
> 4. **Fixed dates.** ExCo dates and deadlines are not moved at short notice. Work for events is budgeted separately
>    from the Chapter Manager's base agreement.
> 5. **Briefing before taking office.** Every new officer receives a briefing from the Chapter Manager and our AI
>    house rules before they start.
>
> Cost: the rules cost no money; they ask for discipline. Reviewed each year at the officer handover.
>
> Decided on: {{DATE_OF_DECISION}} · Minutes: {{LINK_OR_REFERENCE}}

---

## Why this is not an AI question

AI makes drafting, filing and reporting faster. It cannot decide who may ask the Chapter Manager for what, or by when.
Several officers sending urgent requests through several channels will outrun any tool. These guardrails are about how
the ExCo works — the Chair sets them, and AI then makes them easy to live by: one request list your assistant can
sort, briefings it can draft from the folder, reminders that go out a week ahead.

## How to bring this up with your Chair — as a partner

You are not asking for less work. You are offering the Chair a way to get more out of the hours the chapter already pays for.

**Choose the setting.** A 1:1 with the Chair, ideally before the first ExCo of the year or when a new Chair takes over.
Not in a full ExCo meeting — the Chair should own the proposal.

**Start from what the Chair wants.**
> "I'd like the ExCo to get faster, cleaner support from me this year. I've drafted five simple rules for how
> we work together — may I show you?"

**Describe what you see, without blame.**
> "When requests come from several officers at once, I end up guessing the priority. A single list from you would let
> me deliver the most important things first."

**Offer your side of the deal.** Rule 3 binds you too: 24 hours or a date. Say it first.
> "I commit to answering within 24 hours or giving a date. In return it would help me to have material a week ahead."

**Hand over the text, let the Chair shape it.**
> "Here's a draft. Change whatever doesn't fit us — it's your call whether it goes to the ExCo."

**Pair it with the AI one-pager** ([ai-use-one-pager.md](ai-use-one-pager.md)). Together they make one agenda item:
how our chapter works, with and without AI.

**Follow up in writing** — ask your assistant for a short thank-you mail that summarises what the Chair decided and
what you will prepare. You send it yourself.

```
Draft a short, warm follow-up to {{CHAIR_ROLE}} after our 1:1 on the operating guardrails.
Summarise what we agreed, what the Chair wants to change, and what I will prepare for the ExCo, with the date.
Use only my notes below; mark anything else [verify]. I send it myself.
---
{{YOUR_NOTES}}
```

If the answer is "not this year", keep the text in `02-chapter-knowledge/` and bring it to the next Chair handover
([../05-officer-handover/handover-checklist.md](../05-officer-handover/handover-checklist.md), section C).
