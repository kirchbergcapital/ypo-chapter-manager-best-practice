# Email handoff — mail in, filed, logged, drafted

Purpose: every mail worth keeping ends up in the right folder with a clear name, leaves one line in the log, and gets a reply draft you send yourself.
Time: about 20 minutes to set up the first time; after that about 5 minutes per mail at Level 1, about 2 at Level 2.

## The pattern

```
mail arrives
   → 1. folder + file name       06-communication/inbox-filed/YYYY-MM-DD_sender-role_topic.md
   → 2. one log line             00-handover/log.md
   → 3. reply draft              06-communication/drafts/ (Level 1) or your mailbox drafts (Level 2)
   → 4. YOU read, edit, send
   → 5. if it changed a fact     update 02-chapter-knowledge (chapter-facts.md, a playbook, FAQ.md)
```

Folder names and file names as in [`../02-your-assistant/folder-structure.md`](../02-your-assistant/folder-structure.md).

Why it pays off: three months later, you — or a stand-in, or a new officer — find the mail by date and role,
and the log shows what happened without opening a single inbox.

## Level 1 — copy and paste (any paid AI project)

Your assistant has the house rules: `house-rules.md` in the chapter folder or the project's files, and the core in its instructions ([`../02-your-assistant/cm-assistant-instructions.md`](../02-your-assistant/cm-assistant-instructions.md)).
Copy the mail (sender, date, subject, body) and paste it under this prompt — the same one as Lab B in
[`../02-your-assistant/lab-build-your-assistant.md`](../02-your-assistant/lab-build-your-assistant.md). If the mail asks about a rule, paste the bylaws section under the second `---`;
if it touches no rule, write `no rules needed` there.

```
New email below, followed by our rules. 1) Say which folder it belongs in and give a file name with its extension (YYYY-MM-DD_sender-role_topic.md).
2) Give me one log line for 00-handover/log.md.
3) Draft a reply in my voice; answer only from the rules below and cite the section; mark every fact without a source as [verify]. I send it myself.
---
{{PASTE_EMAIL}}
---
{{PASTE_BYLAWS_EXCERPT}}
```

Then:
1. Save the mail text under the suggested name — ending in `.md` — in `06-communication/inbox-filed/`. Attachments go next to it, same date prefix.
2. Paste the log line at the bottom of `00-handover/log.md`.
3. Copy the draft into your mail program, check every `[verify]`, send.

## Level 2 — with a mail connector

The assistant reads the mail in your mailbox and leaves the reply as a **draft in your mailbox**. You open it, edit, send.

|---|---|---|---|

Prompt for Level 2 (the assistant also needs access to your chapter folder — see [`../01-foundations/three-levels.md`](../01-foundations/three-levels.md)):

```
Read the newest mail from {{SENDER_OR_SUBJECT}} in my mailbox.
1) Save its text to 06-communication/inbox-filed/ as YYYY-MM-DD_sender-role_topic.md (attachments next to it, same prefix).
2) Append one line to 00-handover/log.md: YYYY-MM-DD · topic · source file · what changed · next step.
3) Before drafting, read this thread including my sent replies in it: if I have already answered, say so and draft
   only what is still open. If you cannot see my sent mail, say UNCHECKED. Skip anything with Forum content. Create a reply DRAFT in my mailbox in my voice. Mark facts without a source [verify]. Do not send.
4) If the mail changes a fact in 02-chapter-knowledge, show me the change before you make it.
Show me the file path, the log line and the first three lines of the draft.
```

# One channel to the Chapter Manager

Purpose: all requests from the ExCo reach you through one agreed channel, so you get one prioritised list instead of five competing ones.
Time: one conversation with your Chair, one friendly message to the officers.

## The problem, from your side of the desk

A familiar pattern across chapters: several officers each send their own requests — by mail, WhatsApp, a call in the evening.
Each request is reasonable. Together they collide, and nobody sees the whole list except you.
You end up setting the chapter's priorities without having been asked to.

## From both sides of the table

| | |
|---|---|
| **The chair's view** | A chair rarely sees how many requests reach the Chapter Manager in a week. One channel makes the load visible — and gives the chair the job of setting priorities, which is where that job belongs |
| **The regional view** | Across many chapters, the same lesson holds one level up: when regional mail goes to everyone on every channel, it gets lost. One main channel per audience works better |
| **What made it better** | One channel, one weekly list, one person who decides when two requests collide |

## What "one channel" means

| Element | Agreement |
|---|---|
| **The channel** | One place where ExCo requests reach the CM: a role mailbox (`manager@<chapter-domain>`), or one named thread. Best is a channel that leaves a record in the chapter folder, so the weekly list writes itself |
| **The gate** | Requests go via the Chair or a named person, who sends you a prioritised list |
| **The rhythm** | One list a week, at a fixed time. Urgent means: today, with a reason |
| **Collisions** | When two requests compete, the Chair decides — not you |

This is rule 1 of the operating guardrails in [`../06-chapter-rules/operating-guardrails.md`](../06-chapter-rules/operating-guardrails.md).
The guardrails cost no money; they ask for discipline. Your Chair can propose them at the next ExCo.

## How to introduce it — friendly, not as a complaint

**Step 1 — Talk to your Chair first (10 minutes).** Bring one example week: how many requests, how many channels.
Your assistant can count them from `00-handover/log.md`:

```
Read 00-handover/log.md for the last 4 weeks. Count the requests from officers by channel (mail, WhatsApp, call, other)
and by officer role. Show a small table and the two weeks with the most collisions. Roles only, no names.
```

**Step 2 — The Chair (or you, with the Chair's OK) sends one message to the officers:**

```text
Subject: How we work with {{CM_NAME}} this year

Dear all,

To make sure every request gets the attention it deserves, we are trying one simple rule this year:
please send requests for {{CM_NAME}} to {{CHANNEL — e.g. manager@chapter-domain}}, or to me.
Every {{WEEKDAY}} {{CM_NAME}} receives one prioritised list, and when two things compete, I decide.

Urgent things still work — just say why it is urgent.

Thank you — this gives all of us faster answers.
{{CHAIR_NAME}}
```

**Step 3 — Your weekly list (5 minutes with your assistant):**

```
Read the requests filed since {{LAST_WEEK_DATE}} in 06-communication/inbox-filed/ and the log.
Make one list: request · from (role) · due · effort (S/M/L) · conflicts with.
Sort by due date. Mark collisions for the Chair. Draft a short message to the Chair with the list. I send it.
```

**Step 4 — Keep it kind when someone forgets.** A one-line reply does it:
*"Thank you! I've added it to this week's list for {{CHANNEL}} — {{CHAIR_ROLE}} will confirm the priority."*

## The weekly officer note

One channel brings requests in. The weekly officer note sends one picture back out — once a week, for example on Friday, to all officers on the agreed channel:

| Part | What it says |
|---|---|
| **What went out** | Messages and invitations sent this week |
| **What was agreed** | Decisions, each with its line in `00-handover/decisions.md` |
| **What was paid** | Amounts, each with its receipt or invoice |
| **What went quiet** | Requests and threads still waiting for an answer, with the owner (role) |

Your assistant drafts it from the log, the decisions file and the receipts list — prompt 22 in [`../08-prompts/prompt-library.md`](../08-prompts/prompt-library.md).
You read it, change what needs changing, and send it. Officers stop asking "what did we decide?", and the note itself lands in the chapter folder.

## Members are different

One channel is for **requests from the ExCo**. Members can still reach you the way they like. For members, the habit is:
mail for what is binding (dates, payments, registrations), WhatsApp for what is short (reminders, quick questions).
Patterns for both: [message-templates.md](message-templates.md) · [whatsapp.md](whatsapp.md).

# WhatsApp — read faster, answer better, send yourself

Purpose: turn busy WhatsApp chats into a summary, a list of open questions and one-to-one reply drafts — in two stages, from "works today" to "runs on your laptop".
Time: 10 minutes a week at stage 1; building your own app for stage 2 takes one to two hours the first time, with your AI tool (the build itself takes minutes).

Tool facts: as of 21 Oct 2026, with sources; `[verify]` where we could not confirm on the provider's page.

**Your number, your chats.** You work with members from your own WhatsApp number. The chats stay on your phone and stay yours —
see [`../05-officer-handover/what-stays-yours.md`](../05-officer-handover/what-stays-yours.md).
What the chapter needs to keep — who comes, what was decided, what changed — goes from the chat into the chapter folder as a short summary:
**export → summary → file.** That way the chapter's memory is complete, and your phone stays your own.

## Forum stays out

> Forum is sacred. We use AI for Forum logistics only: dates, places, headcounts, costs, deadlines. Never for what is said in or around a Forum: topics, stories, notes, retreat conversations, anything shared in confidence, or any link between a name and a topic. If in doubt, leave it out.

For WhatsApp that means:

- **Forum groups, and any chat with Forum content, are never exported, uploaded or summarised.**
- **A mixed chat** — logistics and Forum in one thread — is not exported either. Type the logistics (date, place, headcount) into the event file by hand.
- **Forum content in an export by accident?** Delete the export. Do not summarise it.
- The prompt below starts with a stop line for this, and so does prompt 12 in the library.

## The two WhatsApp stages

The WhatsApp stages are not the kit's Levels 1–3: stage 1 works at any level; stage 2 needs an AI tool that can write and run code on your laptop (Level 3).

| Stage | What | Setup | Good for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1 · Chat export** | WhatsApp's own "Export chat" → a text file → your assistant | None | Every CM, today |
| **2 · Your own small WhatsApp app** | You build it with your AI tool, following [`09-whatsapp-app-build-guide`](../09-whatsapp-app-build-guide/): it reads the chats you choose, summarises them and drafts replies | One to two hours the first time, Level 3 | CMs with many active groups |

At every stage: **the assistant reads and drafts. You decide what goes out — one message at a time**, from your phone or, with the optional send step of your own app, after you confirm that one message.

## Stage 1 — Export chat (official, works today)

1. In WhatsApp, open the chat → menu → **More → Export chat** → *without media* `[verify: menu path differs on iPhone and Android]`.
2. Keep the export outside the chapter folder — on your phone, or in your laptop's Downloads folder — named `YYYY-MM-DD_chat-name_topic.txt`, e.g. `2026-11-14_event-group_holiday-dinner.txt`. The chapter folder gets only the summary (below).
3. Upload it to your AI project (Level 1) or give your assistant the file (Level 2), and use this prompt:

```
Stop and tell me if the chat contains Forum content; do not summarise it.
Read the WhatsApp export {{FILE}} from {{DATE_FROM}} onwards.
1) Who confirmed, who declined, who has not answered — for {{EVENT}}. Names exactly as in chapter-facts.md
   or the event attendee list; if a name only appears as a phone number, write "unknown number", never guess.
2) Open questions that need my answer, each with the date it was asked.
3) Anything that changes a fact (date, venue, cost) — show it, do not update files yet.
4) One-to-one reply drafts for the open questions, short, in the language of each person. I send them myself.
5) A short summary for the event folder: RSVPs, decisions, changed facts — each with the date of the message.
6) One log line for 00-handover/log.md.
```

Save the summary in the event folder (`03-events/{{EVENT_FOLDER}}/YYYY-MM-DD_whatsapp-summary.md`). That file is what the chapter keeps; the chat stays on your phone, and you can delete the export afterwards.
The summary is a decision record: RSVPs, decisions, changed facts, each with its date. Names appear only with a role or an RSVP — never next to a topic.

**What good looks like:** a three-column attendance table, a short list of open questions, a few drafts of two or three lines each, and a summary of five lines or fewer for the event folder.
A fictional export to practise with: [`../examples/whatsapp-export-event-group.txt`](../examples/whatsapp-export-event-group.txt).

**Group posts:** if one message should go to the whole event group, ask for one group post — you post it yourself in the group.

## Stage 2 — Build your own small WhatsApp app with your AI tool

[`../09-whatsapp-app-build-guide/`](../09-whatsapp-app-build-guide/) is a build guide, not a ready-made program. You give its build prompt to your AI tool
(Level 3, see [`../01-foundations/three-levels.md`](../01-foundations/three-levels.md)), and together you build a small app that runs on your laptop.
It connects the way WhatsApp Web does, **reads** the chats you choose, **summarises** them and **drafts replies**, and saves its exports as Markdown files in its own folder on your laptop, outside the chapter folder.
Your assistant reads such a file exactly like a stage-1 export; only the summary goes into the event folder. Sending is an **optional** step you can add later: the app then sends one message only after you have confirmed that one message. WhatsApp can restrict a number that uses an unofficial client, and your chapter's or YPO's WhatsApp rules apply to both stages — where messages may not leave a group, that group is not exported.

The app uses Baileys, an open-source library that is not affiliated with WhatsApp; its own README discourages bulk or automated messaging
([npm: baileys](https://www.npmjs.com/package/baileys)).

WhatsApp's [messaging guidelines](https://www.whatsapp.com/legal/messaging-guidelines) (last updated 23 Sep 2026) ask users not to
"use unofficial clients, bulk messaging, auto-messaging, auto-dialing, or automation to harm WhatsApp or our users". The build guide keeps your app far from that line:
it reads at your own pace, drafts, and sends — if you add sending at all — only the single message you have just confirmed. The full rules for Europe: WhatsApp's [terms for the European Region](https://www.whatsapp.com/legal/terms-of-service-eea).

### Good habits

| Habit | Why it helps |
|---|---|
| **You confirm every send** | The app reads, summarises and drafts. If you add sending, each message goes out only after you confirm that one message — no bulk, no schedules, no automatic replies |
| **Personal pace** | Export a few chats when you sit down to work — not every minute, not all chats |
| **Keep what the chapter needs in the chapter folder** | Summarise decisions and RSVPs from WhatsApp into the event file — the chat itself stays on your phone |
| **Leave out what is not yours to read** | Forum groups, chats with Forum content and private conversations are never exported |

## A group charter — pin it in every group you run

A group runs more smoothly when everyone knows what it is for. Post this as the first message of each group you set up, pin it,
and keep a copy in `06-communication/templates/`. Adapt the purpose line; keep the rest.

```text
{{GROUP_NAME}} — what this group is for

Purpose: {{e.g. logistics for the holiday dinner — times, places, quick questions}}.
Everyone here said yes before being added. Know someone who should join? Ask them first, then tell me.
Your phone number is visible to everyone in this group.
Please share no sensitive documents here — no ID copies, contracts or bank details.
Decisions and payments go by mail, so the chapter has them on file. This chat is for the quick things.
Questions about the group: {{YOUR_NAME}}, Chapter Manager.
```

**Ask, then add.** Before you add someone, send a short one-to-one message — *"May I add you to the {{EVENT}} group?"* — and add the people who say yes.
Your assistant can draft these one-to-ones from the RSVP list; you send them.

|---|---|

Ready-made prompt: "Chat-export summary" in [`../08-prompts/prompt-library.md`](../08-prompts/prompt-library.md).

Same rules: your choice of chats, never a Forum group, exports outside the chapter folder.

# Knowledge base

> What your chapter knows, in one folder, in plain files your assistant can read. So "how do we do X?" gets the same answer in March as in October — and a stand-in or the next officer finds it without calling you.

---

## 1 · What it is

Your knowledge base is the folder `02-chapter-knowledge` inside the chapter folder ([`../02-your-assistant/folder-structure.md`](../02-your-assistant/folder-structure.md)).
It holds what is **true and reusable**. Your assistant reads it before it answers. Everything else in the chapter folder — emails, exports, drafts — is a source that feeds it.

## 2 · What goes in

| File | What it holds | Starts from |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `chapter-facts.md` | The chapter on one page: year, officers, standard events, budget lines, channels, deadlines | [`../02-your-assistant/chapter-facts-interview.md`](../02-your-assistant/chapter-facts-interview.md) |
| `game-plan.md` | The chapter's goals from the Game Plan: metric, tracking source, owner (role), status, evidence. You track it; the chapter commits; the Chair owns delivery | The empty file in [`../02-your-assistant/template-folder/02-chapter-knowledge/game-plan.md`](../02-your-assistant/template-folder/02-chapter-knowledge/game-plan.md) · [`../05-officer-handover/game-plan-keeping.md`](../05-officer-handover/game-plan-keeping.md) |
| Bylaws | The chapter's bylaws: the original PDF **and** a Markdown copy with page numbers | The current signed version |
| Operating guidelines | How the chapter works in practice: who approves what, lead times, the one channel to the Chapter Manager | [`../06-chapter-rules/operating-guardrails.md`](../06-chapter-rules/operating-guardrails.md) |
| `FAQ.md` | Questions members and officers ask again and again — each with a short answer and its source | The last three questions you answered twice |
| Event playbooks | One file per standard event: timeline back from the date, venue and supplier notes, run sheet, checklist, budget line | Your best past event folder in `03-events` — the steps in [`event-lifecycle.md`](event-lifecycle.md) |
| Learnings, one file per role | `learnings-chapter-manager.md`, `learnings-learning-officer.md`, `learnings-membership-officer.md` … dated lines: what worked, what did not, what we change | The prompt in [`keeping-it-true.md`](keeping-it-true.md) after each event |
| Process playbooks | One file per recurring process you run every year, such as `process-board-registration.md` (incoming board registration, membership transfer, platform steps, a tool's how-to with dated workarounds) | Your own last run plus the official guidance |
| Platform field map | `platform-fields.md` — each screen field points at the chapter file that already holds the value | The first screen that asks — prompt 30 |

What stays in other folders: single emails (`06-communication`), exports (`07-reporting`), event working files (`03-events`), drafts.
What never goes in: passwords, members' private details (home addresses, ID or passport copies, health or family details), Forum content. The chapter's password manager, YPO Connect and the Forum itself are the right homes for those — the Forum rule in full: [`../02-your-assistant/folder-structure.md`](../02-your-assistant/folder-structure.md).

## 3 · Markdown, not a database

Write the knowledge base as Markdown files (`.md`): plain text with headings, lists and tables.

| Markdown files | A database or a new app |
| --- | --- |
| Every AI assistant reads them, at every level | Needs a connector, a licence, or an export |
| You read and edit them in any editor | Needs someone who knows the tool |
| They move with the folder at handover | Often tied to one person's login |
| A new tool next year reads the same files | A new tool next year means a migration |

Lists that look like tables — receipts, attendance — can be CSV files. Your assistant reads those too.

**Turning a PDF into Markdown.** Keep the original. Add a Markdown copy next to it:

```
Turn the attached {{DOCUMENT}} into Markdown for 02-chapter-knowledge.
Keep every heading and section number. Put the page number in brackets after each section, like (p. 4).
Change no wording. Mark anything you could not read as [verify].
At the top write: Source: {{FILE_NAME}}, version/date {{DATE}}, converted {{TODAY}}.
```

Then check three sections against the PDF yourself. If they match, the copy is good.

## 4 · One truth

Each fact lives in **one** file. Other files point to it.

- The date of the holiday dinner lives in `chapter-facts.md`. The playbook says "date: see chapter-facts.md".
- A rule lives in the bylaws copy. `FAQ.md` quotes it and names the section.
- Who holds a role lives in the officer table. Nowhere else.

Find the same fact in two places? Keep one, make the other a pointer. If the two disagree, [`keeping-it-true.md`](keeping-it-true.md) shows how to settle it.

## 5 · Where it lives: cloud, synced to your laptop from Level 2 on

**Recommendation:** keep the chapter folder in the chapter's cloud storage (Google Drive or OneDrive). At Level 1 that is all — you upload the key files to your AI project.
From Level 2 on, install the storage's desktop app so the same folder is synced to your laptop.

| Why cloud | Why also on the laptop |
| --- | --- |
| The chapter owns it, through a role account | Desktop AI apps (Level 2) open the folder directly — no uploading |
| Two admins, so nobody is the only key | Level 3 (Claude Code) works locally in the same folder |
| Backed up and shareable with officers | You work offline on a train; it syncs later |

At Level 1, you upload the key files (`chapter-facts.md`, `FAQ.md`, the bylaws copy, `handover.md`) into your AI project and refresh them when they change.
At Levels 2 and 3, the assistant reads and writes the synced folder itself. Same files, same structure — see [`../01-foundations/three-levels.md`](../01-foundations/three-levels.md) and [`../01-foundations/where-your-data-lives.md`](../01-foundations/where-your-data-lives.md).

One person edits a file at a time. Officers read; you and your assistant write.

## 6 · Asking it questions

```
{{QUESTION}}
Answer only from 02-chapter-knowledge. Quote file and section (and page for the bylaws).
If the files do not cover it, say so, write [verify with Regional Manager], and suggest where the answer should live once we have it.
```

When the answer is new and worth keeping, add it to `FAQ.md` with its source. Next time the question costs you nothing.

## 7 · From both seats

Patterns as chairs and regional officers tend to see them — no single chapter described.

| | Pattern |
| --- | --- |
| What worked | A reusable run sheet per event, kept and improved from year to year instead of rebuilt. |
| What did not | Three places to file things: a private inbox, a private drive, a WhatsApp chat. Every officer change started from zero. |
| What we improved | One chapter storage, one fixed structure, one file per fact. New officers read the folder before they ask. |


## 8 · Meetings: record them

Recording your chapter meetings — ExCo, officer calls, committee and planning calls — is the easiest way to keep what was said and decided.
YPO's Zoom records without extra tools; where your account also shows a transcript or an AI summary, keep that too `[verify for your account]`.

- **Say it at the start:** "We are recording; the notes will be taken from the recording." People know where the notes come from.
- **Save the source, keep the truth:** the transcript or summary goes with its date into `07-reporting/` or the event folder. Prompt 3 turns it into action items, a minutes draft and lines for `00-handover/decisions.md`.
- **Forum meetings are never recorded or transcribed.** Forum is sacred.
- Where your chapter or YPO has rules for recordings and AI notetakers, they apply.

# Keeping it true

> A knowledge base is only useful while it is right. Four habits keep it right with little effort: sources feed the truth, open points are marked, logs only grow, and twenty minutes a month you check.

---

## 1 · Sources and truth

| | What it is | Examples | Rule |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Truth** | Your own files, kept current | `chapter-facts.md`, bylaws copy, `FAQ.md`, playbooks, learnings, `handover.md` | Edited in place the same day; every change gets a log line |
| **Sources** | What comes in from outside | Emails, WhatsApp exports, PDFs, exports from YPO tools, meeting notes | Saved unchanged with a date; cited when they change the truth |
| **History** | What happened and what was decided | `00-handover/log.md`, `00-handover/decisions.md` | Append-only |

The flow is always the same: **a source arrives → you or your assistant cite it → the truth is updated → one log line.**
An email that changes the venue does not "become" the truth. It is the reason the playbook changes.

## 2 · Marks that keep you honest

| Mark | Means | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `[verify]` | A fact the assistant could not source, or you were not sure of | Confirm with the owner, then replace the mark with the fact and its source |
| `[verify with Regional Manager]` | A rule question your files do not answer | Ask once; put the answer in `FAQ.md` with its source |
| `UNCHECKED` | A file could not be opened, or a reply has not come | Not fine, not failing — open. Check before anyone relies on it |
| `SOURCE MISSING` | A draft says something in a person's name without a file line or a message behind it | Find the source or take the line out before you send |

A mark is a tool, not a failure. It shows the next person exactly where the work is.

To see all of them at once:

```
List every [verify], UNCHECKED and SOURCE MISSING in the chapter folder.
For each: file, line, what is missing, and who could confirm it (role).
```

## 3 · Logs only grow

`log.md` and `decisions.md` are append-only. New lines go at the bottom. Nothing is rewritten.

A correction is a new line that points to the old one (fictional example):

```
2026-11-03 · holiday dinner · venue email · start time set to 19:00 · send invitation
2026-11-05 · holiday dinner · venue email 2026-11-05 · correction of 2026-11-03: start time 19:30 · update invitation draft
```

Why it matters: a year from now, a new Chair asks "why did we move the dinner?". The log answers in one line, with the source. Nobody has to remember.

`handover.md` is the one page you may rewrite. It shows the current state and points to the log for history.

## 4 · The monthly check — 20 minutes

Put a repeating 20-minute slot in your calendar, for example the first working Monday of the month.

| Min | Step |
| --- | --- |
| 0–5 | Run the prompt below |
| 5–15 | Decide each proposal: yes, no, or ask someone. Clear the [verify] marks you can clear right now |
| 15–20 | Let the assistant apply the yes-items, add log lines, and refresh `handover.md` |

```
Monthly check. Read 02-chapter-knowledge, 00-handover, and the log lines of the last 30 days.
1) List every [verify] and UNCHECKED, with who could clear it (role).
2) Officers, dates or events that changed in the log or in 06-communication / 07-reporting but not in chapter-facts.md. Cite both files.
3) Facts that appear in two files with different values. Cite both.
4) Files in the wrong folder, or names that break YYYY-MM-DD_topic.
5) Learnings lines from the last month that should change a playbook.
Propose every edit as a list. Change nothing until I say yes.
Then apply the approved edits, add one log line per change, and refresh 00-handover/handover.md.
```

## 5 · After every event: learnings

Within two days of each event, while it is fresh. It takes five minutes and it is the single best gift to next year's officers.
Working with the event template? The longer debrief with numbers and hours is prompt 18 — see [`event-lifecycle.md`](event-lifecycle.md).

```
Event done: {{EVENT}} on {{DATE}}. My notes: {{NOTES}}
(attendance, what members said, what went wrong, what to keep, costs against budget).
1) Append 3–7 dated lines to 02-chapter-knowledge/learnings-{{ROLE}}.md, each as: what worked · what did not · what we change next time.
2) Propose edits to the playbook for this event in 02-chapter-knowledge. Mark them as proposals.
3) Give me one log line for 00-handover/log.md.
4) Draft a thank-you to the host or speaker for me to send. Use no member's feedback by name.
```

Ask the officer who owned the event to add their own lines to their learnings file — or send you three bullet points and let the assistant append them.
When the role changes, the next officer reads that file first.

## 6 · When two sources disagree

It happens every month: the playbook says one thing, a new email says another. Settle it in five steps.

1. **Put both side by side**, each with its source and date. Your assistant does this when you ask: *"These two disagree. Show both with source and date."*
2. **Ask who owns the fact.** Newer is not automatically right. The source from the owner wins:

   | Kind of fact | Who owns it |
   | --- | --- |
   | Rules and bylaws | The current signed bylaws; YPO guidance from its official source, with its date |
   | How the chapter does things | The ExCo, through a line in `decisions.md` |
   | Event details | The venue's or supplier's latest written confirmation |
   | Who holds a role | The officer table in `chapter-facts.md`, confirmed by the Chair |
   | Money | The budget file and the invoice |

3. **Cannot tell?** Mark the fact `[verify]`, ask the owner in one short message (your assistant drafts it), and add an open item to `handover.md`.
4. **Decided?** Update the truth file, add a line to `decisions.md` if it was a decision, and a log line that names both sources.
5. **Fix the cause.** If the old value came from a playbook, update the playbook too, so it does not come back next year.

## 7 · At the officer change

If you kept these habits, the handover is the folder: `handover.md` on top, the logs for history, the learnings files for each role.

# Event lifecycle — from first idea to archive

> Events come back all year, and each one touches many people and files. One event folder, the same eight files every time, and a fixed rhythm from T-90 to T+14:
> your assistant finds every event fact in the same place, and the next organiser starts from your folder instead of from zero.

---

## 1 · The event folder

Copy `03-events/_event-template/` from your chapter folder ([`../02-your-assistant/template-folder/03-events/_event-template/`](../02-your-assistant/template-folder/03-events/_event-template/) in the kit) and rename it `YYYY-MM-DD_event-name`.

| File | What it holds |
| --- | --- |
| `brief.md` | Why, for whom, format, date, target number, who decides (by role), what success looks like — the event's one truth |
| `budget.md` | The budget line from `05-finance`, commitments, actuals — amounts only from a contract, an invoice or a receipt |
| `run-of-show.md` | Minute plan with roles, tech and plan B — after the event, the candidate for the playbook |
| `speaker.md` | Bio, briefing, what was agreed, tech and travel needs — nothing without a source |
| `venue.md` | Contract, capacity, deadlines, contact by role, deliveries, parking |
| `rsvps.md` | One table: person as on the list · status · guests · venue note the member agreed to share · source and date · attended |
| `comms-log.md` | Append-only, one line per item: date · channel · topic · open point · hours — every line ends like `· 0.5h` |
| `debrief.md` | Worked · did not work · change · numbers · hours — the source for the learnings files |

Optional: `follow-up.md` for open loops after the event, and `YYYY-MM-DD_whatsapp-summary.md` for what the chapter keeps from the event group — the chat itself stays on your phone ([`../03-communication/whatsapp.md`](../03-communication/whatsapp.md)).

## 2 · From T-90 to T+14

T = the event day. Adjust the distances to your chapter; keep the order.

| When | What | Lands in | Prompt |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| T-90 | Clarify the brief with the officer in charge, block the date, create the folder | `brief.md` | — |
| T-75 | Venue requests and deadlines | `venue.md`, `budget.md` | 11 Email handoff |
| T-60 | Speaker: research, request, confirm | `speaker.md` | 6 Speaker research |
| T-45 | Invitation draft, approval | `rsvps.md` (header) | Template 1 in [`../03-communication/message-templates.md`](../03-communication/message-templates.md) |
| T-30 | Run-of-show draft, budget check | `run-of-show.md`, `budget.md` | 17 Run of show · 4 Budget check |
| T-21 | Speaker briefing — your assistant drafts, you send | `speaker.md` | 15 Speaker briefing |
| T-14 … T-3 | RSVP reconciliation, weekly, then daily | `rsvps.md` | 16 RSVP reconciliation |
| T-7 | Final number to the venue | `venue.md` | — |
| T-1 | Checklist, mails to the roles involved, checkpoint | `comms-log.md`, `00-handover/handover.md` | 13 Checkpoint |
| T-0 | Attendance | `rsvps.md`, column "attended" | — |
| T+2 | Thanks, close the loops | `follow-up.md` | 1 Event follow-up |
| T+7 | Debrief with numbers and hours | `debrief.md`, `02-chapter-knowledge/learnings-{{ROLE}}.md` | 18 Debrief → learnings |
| T+14 | Reconcile invoices, move the folder to `09-archive` | `budget.md` (final) | 4 Budget check |

All prompts: [`../08-prompts/prompt-library.md`](../08-prompts/prompt-library.md) — group G · Event lifecycle (15–18), the others by number.

## 3 · Make event hours visible

Event work comes in waves, and it is easy for it to disappear into "the job". A few small habits make it visible — to you first.

| Habit | How |
| --- | --- |
| **Hours in the comms log** | Each line in `comms-log.md` ends with your hours, for example `· 0.5h`. You write them, or confirm the assistant's suggestion; only hours you confirm count |
| **Hours in the debrief** | `debrief.md` has an "Hours" block per phase — and a line for the base work you postponed meanwhile |
| **Base work stays separate** | Everyday chapter work stays in `00-handover/log.md`, without an hours column — your hours for it go into your own hours log, by area ([`../10-time-by-area/`](../10-time-by-area/)) |
| **A conversation about facts** | Your hours per event are on file. When you share a debrief with your Chair, scope and event fees become a conversation about what the work really took |
| **Your own pattern** | After three events, ask your assistant for the pattern across your own debriefs — and only your own |

No benchmark numbers here on purpose: your chapter, your events, your log. The bet: the hours come from searching and answering twice. Test it with your own week ([log](../10-time-by-area/)).

```
Read the debrief.md files of my last three events in 03-events/ and 09-archive/.
Show hours per phase for each event, side by side, and the base work I postponed.
What pattern do you see? Only from these files — no comparison with other chapters.
```

## 4 · After the event: into the knowledge base

# Handover checklists — officer, Chapter Manager, Chair

Three short lists for the three handovers a chapter goes through. Copy the one you need into
`00-handover/` (for example `00-handover/YYYY-MM_learning-officer.md`) and let your assistant fill in what the
folder already knows.

When officer terms start and end is set by your bylaws — your Chair or Regional Manager can confirm it for your chapter.

**Two habits that make every handover below easier:**
- **Meet early.** A common recommendation for associations: hold the handover meeting at least two weeks before the
  term ends, so there is time for follow-up questions — and record it or name a notetaker
  (for example in [ASAE's guide to officer handovers](https://www.asaecenter.org/resources/articles/an_plus/2026/09-september/a-guide-to-seamless-officer-handovers-for-associations), Sept 2026).
- **Hand over the risks, not only the documents.** Lists that only move files miss what someone was promised.
  Each checklist has its own line for unfinished risks and open promises.

---

## A · Officer change (Learning, Forum, Membership, any officer role)

| # | What | Done when | Who |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The role on one page** — tasks, the year at a glance, what went well, what to change | One page in `00-handover/` (use [officer-briefing-template.md](officer-briefing-template.md)) | Outgoing officer + CM |
| 2 | **Access** — add the new officer, remove the outgoing one (role address, chapter folder, event tools, groups) | Access list in `01-role-and-calendar/` is current | CM + admin |
| 3 | **Open projects and contracts with a deadline** — venues, speakers, sponsors, anything signed | Each item has an owner and a date | Outgoing officer |
| 4 | **Unfinished risks and open promises** — what could still go wrong, and what someone was promised (a speaker, a sponsor, a member), even if nothing is signed | Each item has an owner and a date | Outgoing officer |
| 5 | **Commitment deadlines of the role** — the ones this role owns, as listed in the official source | Entered in your commitments calendar ([template](commitments-calendar.md)) | CM |
| 6 | **Contacts and the one channel to the CM** — who the officer works with, and how requests reach you | Written in the briefing | Chair + CM |
| 7 | **30-minute handover call** — new officer, CM, outgoing officer; at least two weeks before the term ends | Call held and recorded or noted; notes in `00-handover/log.md` | CM invites |
| 8 | **Passwords only in the chapter's password manager** — never in mails, chats or files | Vault entries moved to the new officer | Admin |

**Prompt for your assistant:**

```
Prepare the handover for the incoming {{ROLE}}. Read 00-handover/handover.md, 01-role-and-calendar/,
03-events/ and 00-handover/log.md. Fill the eight points of the officer checklist in
05-officer-handover/handover-checklist.md (section A). For every open item give owner, date and what
"done" looks like. Mark anything you could not find as UNCHECKED. Draft only.
```

## B · Chapter Manager change — what your successor (or a stand-in) needs on day one

Write this list while you are still in the role — it also carries a stand-in through a few weeks of absence.
The chapter keeps the facts; your method stays with you ([what-stays-yours.md](what-stays-yours.md)).

**Access (day one)**
- [ ] manager@ mailbox — password reset by an admin, not passed on in a chat
- [ ] Chapter cloud folder — your successor becomes admin, together with the Chair
- [ ] Chapter password manager — vault handed over by the Chair or the second admin
- [ ] YPO tools (Chapter Management Hub, Cvent, YPO Connect, Zoom, Canva) — ask the Regional Manager how access moves to the new CM **[verify]**

**Orientation (first week)**
- [ ] `00-handover/handover.md` — current, with open items and what "done" looks like
- [ ] `00-handover/log.md` — the history, append-only
- [ ] `02-chapter-knowledge/chapter-facts.md` — chapter name, officer roles, standard events, budget lines, channels, deadlines
- [ ] Officer list with roles and the one channel each officer uses to reach the CM
- [ ] [commitments-calendar.md](commitments-calendar.md) filled for this fiscal year, with status per commitment
- [ ] This year's events with status (date, venue, contract, registration, budget)
- [ ] **Goals:** `02-chapter-knowledge/game-plan.md` — status as of {{DATE}}, with the last quarterly report ([game-plan-keeping.md](game-plan-keeping.md))

**Money and contracts**
- [ ] Budget file and who approves what
- [ ] Open invoices, deposits, sponsor agreements with dates
- [ ] How expenses and receipts are processed
- [ ] **Unfinished risks and open promises** — to members, speakers, sponsors, venues — each with owner and date

**People**
- [ ] Introductions to the Chair, the Past Chair and the Regional Manager
- [ ] A 30-minute handover meeting: you, your successor, the Chair — at least two weeks before your last day, recorded or with notes

**The assistant**
- [ ] The assistant instructions file and the link to the chapter folder — your successor sets up their own AI project in a paid account with training off and points it at the same folder ([../01-foundations/paid-plan-and-training-off.md](../01-foundations/paid-plan-and-training-off.md))
- [ ] The chapter's own texts in `06-communication/templates/` and the prompts its routines use in `08-prompts/`

**What stays with you**
- Your WhatsApp number and chats, your own templates and prompt library, your method and your personal AI plan stay yours; the chapter's AI seat on `manager@` stays with the chapter.
  Before you hand over, write a short summary of any open member conversation into the folder (`06-communication/whatsapp/`: who, topic, what is
  promised, next step) — then your successor can pick it up. The full line, and the 30-minute handover meeting:
  [what-stays-yours.md](what-stays-yours.md).
- Your access to the role accounts, the chapter folder and the password manager ends with the role — the admins
  close it on your last day.

# What stays yours — and what stays with the chapter

**In one sentence:** your know-how is yours; the chapter owns its memory. A clear line between the two protects you,
makes every handover easy, and lets you take your way of working to the next chapter.

**Self-employed or employed?** Chapter Managers work for their chapter — a small association, sometimes a small company — never for YPO Global.
If the chapter employs you, its rules, its AI rules included, and your contract set the details, such as who pays the AI plan and what you take with you `[check your contract]`.

## Why a clear line helps you

- **Your know-how is what you bring.** It grows with every event, every ExCo and every chapter. It belongs to you.
- **The chapter trusts a CM who keeps its memory in its own place.** Chapter data in the chapter folder is the
  strongest sign of a professional setup.
- **No awkward questions at the end.** When the line is clear from day one, the handover itself can be a 30-minute
  meeting.

## Yours

| What | Where it lives |
|---|---|
| Your know-how and experience — how events run, what works with officers, what to watch for | In your head and in your own notes |

| What | Where it lives |

# Game plan keeping — the goals stay alive after the Game Day

**In one sentence:** the chapter commits to its goals on the Game Day; the Chair owns delivery; you keep the file
current — so the goals are still visible in March, and the next Chair finds them where they belong.

Time: 30 minutes a quarter, plus one goals handover a year.

## Who does what

| Role | Part in the game plan |
|---|---|
| **The chapter** | Commits to the goals on the Game Day |
| **The Chair** | Owns delivery of every goal |
| **Officers** | Own the goals assigned to their role |
| **The Chapter Manager** | Tracks and prepares: keeps `game-plan.md` current, drafts the status and the quarterly report |

You do not write goals *for* the chapter, and you do not reword them. When a goal has no metric, no owner or no
tracking source, the file says so and marks it `[verify]` — the Chair decides how to close the gap.

## The file

`02-chapter-knowledge/game-plan.md` is the truth file for the goals. An empty template is in
[`../02-your-assistant/template-folder/02-chapter-knowledge/game-plan.md`](../02-your-assistant/template-folder/02-chapter-knowledge/game-plan.md).

- **Header:** plan year, Game Day date, where the dataset came from, date of the last update.
- **One row per goal:** ID · Goal (action and hard deadline) · Metric, including its denominator · Tracking (which file or source shows progress) · Owner (by role) · Chair owns delivery · Status · Evidence (file and line).
- **Sources stay as they came:** the playbook PDF and the dataset go unchanged into `02-chapter-knowledge/game-plan-sources/`.
- **Standing decisions** from the Game Day go into `00-handover/decisions.md`.

A meta-goal makes the year easy to read — for example *"80 % of the goals met by fiscal year end"* (fictional example;
your chapter sets its own).

## The quarterly slot — 30 minutes

Put four dates in your calendar, one per quarter. Then:

1. **Goal status** (prompt 19) — your assistant reads `game-plan.md`, `03-events/` and `04-members-and-prospects/` and
   proposes a status per goal: met · on track · at risk · UNCHECKED, each with the evidence. You accept or change it.
2. **Quarterly report** (prompt 20) — a one-page draft for the Chair: done, on track, at risk with the reason,
   decisions needed from the ExCo, share of goals met against the meta-goal. Owners named by role.
   Save it as `07-reporting/YYYY-MM-DD_game-plan-Qn.md`.
3. **You send it** to the Chair yourself.
4. **One log line** in `00-handover/log.md`:
   `YYYY-MM-DD · game plan · Q{{n}} status · report to Chair · {{N}} at risk · next check {{DATE}}`

The prompts are in [`../08-prompts/prompt-library.md`](../08-prompts/prompt-library.md), group **H · Game plan**:
19 goal status · 20 quarterly report · 21 goals handover.

## At the handover

When the Chair changes, the goals change owner too: the new Chair now owns delivery.

- **Goals handover** (prompt 21) — per goal: status, what was tried, what is open, next date, owner by role, and which
  goals should be re-confirmed at the first ExCo. No goal dropped, none reworded.
- **`00-handover/handover.md`** carries one line: `Goals: status as of {{DATE}}`.
- **Chair change** — "Goals re-confirmed, owners updated by role" is a line in
  [handover-checklist.md](handover-checklist.md), section C. For a Chapter Manager change, section B lists the goals file.
- **New officers** get the goals their role owns in their one-page briefing
  ([officer-briefing-template.md](officer-briefing-template.md)).

## Why this sits with you

Officers change every year, and the Game Day is one day. The game plan only works if someone keeps it alive after
the Game Day — the person who is still there in the fourth quarter. Thirty minutes a quarter, and the Chair walks into
every ExCo knowing where the chapter stands.

**Start from what the Chair wants.**
> "I'd like the ExCo to get faster, cleaner support from me this year. I've drafted five simple rules for how
> we work together — may I show you?"

**Describe what you see, without blame.**
> "When requests come from several officers at once, I end up guessing the priority. A single list from you would let
> me deliver the most important things first."

**Offer your side of the deal.** Rule 3 binds you too: 24 hours or a date. Say it first.
> "I commit to answering within 24 hours or giving a date. In return it would help me to have material a week ahead."

**Hand over the text, let the Chair shape it.**
> "Here's a draft. Change whatever doesn't fit us — it's your call whether it goes to the ExCo."

**Pair it with the AI one-pager** ([ai-use-one-pager.md](ai-use-one-pager.md)). Together they make one agenda item:
how our chapter works, with and without AI.

**Follow up in writing** — ask your assistant for a short thank-you mail that summarises what the Chair decided and
what you will prepare. You send it yourself.

# Bylaws excerpt · Lakeside Chapter (fictional)

> **Fictional example — not a real chapter, not real rules.** These rules were invented to practise
> "what do our bylaws say about …?" with your assistant. They are not YPO rules and not a template for your bylaws.

Source: Lakeside Chapter Bylaws (fictional), version adopted by the ExCo on 2025-06-12. Excerpt of three sections.

## Section 4.1 · Partners and guests at chapter events

Members may bring their partner to every chapter event unless the invitation says "members only".
Children and other guests may attend events marked "family" in the invitation. For any other event, a guest needs the
approval of the Learning Officer before the RSVP deadline. Guests pay their own share of the event cost.

## Section 4.2 · RSVP deadline

Members reply by the RSVP deadline in the invitation, normally 10 days before the event.
After the deadline, the Chapter Manager may accept a registration only if the venue confirms there is capacity.
A registration after the deadline is confirmed in writing by the Chapter Manager; until then it is a request, not a seat.

## Section 6.1 · Requests to the Chapter Manager and all-member mailings

Requests to the Chapter Manager come from the Chair or from the officer responsible for the topic.
Material for an all-member mailing reaches the Chapter Manager at least one week before the send date.
Shorter notice is possible when the Chair approves it in writing.
