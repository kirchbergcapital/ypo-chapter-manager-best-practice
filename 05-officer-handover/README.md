# 05 · Officer handover — keep the chapter's memory when the ExCo changes

> Workshop block: **PERSIST** (question 3 — *How do I get through the yearly change of officers without losing what we know?*)

Chairs and officers rotate every year. You stay — self-employed or employed by the chapter. That makes you the
natural keeper of what the chapter knows, and this module gives you six tools to do that. The bet: it fits into the hours you already have, because the time comes from searching and answering twice — your log will tell you.

## What is in this folder

| File | Use it when | Time to first result |
|---|---|---|
| [role-accounts.md](role-accounts.md) | You set up the chapter accounts the kit requires — at least `manager@` and `chair@` in Microsoft 365 or Google Workspace — so chapter mail and files belong to the chapter, and you need words for your Chair | 20 min to read, one conversation with your Chair |
| [handover-checklist.md](handover-checklist.md) | An officer, the Chair or you yourself hand over a role | 10 min per handover with your assistant |
| [officer-briefing-template.md](officer-briefing-template.md) | A new officer starts — you give them one page | 5 min with your assistant |
| [commitments-calendar.md](commitments-calendar.md) | You plan the year and want the Chapter Commitment deadlines in one view — an empty template you fill from the official source | 15 min once, then a monthly glance |
| [game-plan-keeping.md](game-plan-keeping.md) | The Game Day is over and the goals need to stay alive — status per goal, a quarterly report for the Chair, and the goals handover | 30 min a quarter |
| [what-stays-yours.md](what-stays-yours.md) | You want a clear line between what is yours (know-how, templates, your WhatsApp number) and the chapter's memory — and a calm handover when an agreement ends | 10 min to read |

The chapter rules that go with this module (AI use, how the ExCo works with you) are in
[../06-chapter-rules/README.md](../06-chapter-rules/README.md).

## The pattern we see across chapters

| | Question 3: officer change |
|---|---|
| **Works well** | The Past Chair stays in the ExCo for a year and is there to ask |
| **Does not work** | The handover is one conversation; deadlines slip when they are not written down |
| **What made it better** | A short checklist, a one-page briefing from the CM to every new officer, agreed guardrails, and one calendar of the commitment deadlines |

## How it fits your assistant

Everything here writes into the folder structure from
[../02-your-assistant/folder-structure.md](../02-your-assistant/folder-structure.md):

- `00-handover/handover.md` — the living handover note (what is set up, open items, what "done" looks like)
- `00-handover/log.md` — append-only log, one dated line per change
- `01-role-and-calendar/` — officer list, the one channel, the commitments calendar
- `02-chapter-knowledge/` — bylaws, charter, strategic plan, chapter-facts.md, game-plan.md

Your assistant follows the house rules in
[../02-your-assistant/template-folder/house-rules.md](../02-your-assistant/template-folder/house-rules.md):
it reads the folder before it answers, marks what it could not check, and drafts — you send.

## In the workshop (Lab C, 4 minutes · 8 if you run it alone)

Ask your assistant for a checkpoint and look at the result:

```
Checkpoint now. Refresh 00-handover/handover.md: what is set up, open items (each with what "done" looks like), paths touched. Then stop.
```

Then two questions:

1. **Could someone continue tomorrow from this file?** If not, the gaps are your next checkpoint.
2. **What in this file could only you have written?** That is your method — it goes with you
   ([what-stays-yours.md](what-stays-yours.md)). The chapter keeps the facts; you keep the way you work.
